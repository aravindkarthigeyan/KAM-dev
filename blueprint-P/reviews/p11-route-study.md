## The actual consumer contract

Both frozen statements fix positive masses, G, ordered distinct positive axes and a positive tube radius η first. They then choose a positive coupling threshold and common `0 < d < D`, before quantifying over every positive smaller coupling. They require a measurable set of finite positive **ambient** phase volume, every point of which belongs to an entire analytic Lagrangian Diophantine torus contained in the same set, and `S ⊆ circularTube η`. P-PLANAR concerns moving-Sun 4-tori in dimension 8; P-FIXED concerns fixed-Sun 2n-tori in dimension 4n. The physical solution and pair-bound clauses are additional assembly work.

This does **not** ask for constants uniform over all Hamiltonians h or all masses. Dependence on the entire fixed analytic Hamiltonian, its chosen complex neighborhood and a selected patch is allowed, because those are determined by the initial physical data. What is forbidden is choosing a new η, pair bounds, or an uncontrolled analytic norm after the coupling. The existing plan's observation that CP09 has “no uniformity in h” is therefore not itself a frozen-contract obstruction.

In the literal canonical convention the joint weak frequency map is `(ν, 2σ)`: Fej04r's quadratic term is `σj(ξj²+ηj²)`, whereas CP09 (8) is `Ωj(uj²+vj²)/2`. Preserve the erratum's sqrt(2) normalization, the canonical coordinate ordering and the period conversion. A fixed invertible scaling of frequency components preserves homogeneous nonplanarity; it does not justify dropping the factor two in the actual Hamiltonian.

### Analyticity and measure

Theorem 4 explicitly promises real-analytic Lagrangian tori and Diophantine flow. Theorem 3(i) supplies analytic symplectic conjugacies for each parameter; parameter regularity is only Lipschitz, which is sufficient. Theorem 3(ii) and (5), with `0 < ε* < meas K`, give positive parameter measure and a bi-Lipschitz map from `K* × T^(d+p)` to its image. A compact parameter set gives a compact, hence measurable, union of whole tori; inverse Lipschitz control transfers positive volume. This is the ambient 2(d+p)-dimensional measure, not the surface measure of one torus. Forward Lipschitz control alone would not suffice.

There are **two** measure steps: Pyartli first selects fast nonresonant actions for preparation; Rüssmann's theorem supplies the final KAM family and its parameter measure. CP09 does not prove the latter by merely citing its Lemma 3. Féjoz's homogeneous sublevel argument can replace the former, after matching domains and uniform constants. It does not replace the analytic KAM input.

### Closeness and uniformity

CP09's statement says only “close to”; Remark 4(ii) describes degeneration as coupling tends to zero. Those words are not a quantitative tube theorem. The proof offers the ingredients: `s ~ (log 1/ε)^(-m)`, fast-action rescaling, equilibrium shift `O(ε log(1/ε)^(2τ0+1))`, polar oscillator actions `O(ε)` and hence Cartesian amplitudes `O(sqrt ε)`, plus the final analytic KAM conjugacy. The oscillator bound is sqrt ε, not ε. A Birkhoff map need not tend to the identity on a fixed ball: its quadratic displacement is small on the shrinking ball, which suffices.

Part 11 must bound the **composed** map in the original `(I,u,v)` coordinates, uniformly over the surviving parameters and all angles. An admissible statement is: for every relatively compact prescribed action neighborhood V in a weakly nondegenerate chart and every `δ > 0`, there is `ε0(V,δ) > 0` such that all `0 < ε < ε0` yield a finite-positive-measure union of analytic tori contained in `T^d × V × {max(|u|,|v|) < δ}`. Equivalently give a displacement function tending to zero, together with a fixed inner action patch. An explicit optimal exponent is unnecessary. Deriving it requires the missing quantitative displacement estimates from the analytic KAM theorem as well as the normal-form estimates; Lemma 8 only controls admissible perturbation size.

Choose the good patch **inside** an arbitrarily small neighborhood of the prescribed axes before ε. A fast Diophantine center can be fixed there, with fixed constants; the finitely many slow nonresonances needed for the selected normalization order are avoided in that patch. The amount/index data and all complex norms then have controlled dependence. This avoids claiming uniform estimates over arbitrary real-analytic functions on a noncompact B, or silently moving the physical center with ε. The physical h and f can be coupling-independent in the zero-coupling chart; the action-only moving/fixed secular difference must be proved in Part 8. No unprinted coupling-dependent version of CP09 is required.

### Regularity, measure and quantitative limits

The exact maps in 38,42,43,59,60 take values in smooth diffeomorphism spaces. A real-analytic Hamiltonian does not turn an arbitrary smooth conjugacy into an analytic map. Fej07e p. 4 explicitly says “Les tores trouvés ici sont de classe C∞” and refers the analytic result to Chierchia–Pusateri/Pusateri's thesis. Thus this route does not match frozen `AnalyticOnNhd ℝ K Set.univ`.

An analytic upgrade could be proved by an analytic twisted-conjugacy theorem with counterterms on a scale of complex strips, quantitative parameter regularity, and the same homogeneous measure argument. That is a new major theorem, not a change of adjectives. Alternatively an analytic a-posteriori regularization theorem might identify analytic tori near selected smooth ones, but this needs analytic approximants with controlled defects, nondegeneracy and an identification/measure argument. A bare appeal to track-K G1 or uniqueness does not give that upgrade for a weakly nondegenerate torus.

Theorem 50 gives, for a homogeneous `(a,b)`-skew curve into R^M, an excluded length bounded by `Cτ,M ((b/a)|I|+1)(γ/a)^(1/M)` for `τ > M²−1`. Theorem 58 extends the argument to parameter domains. Stability of the finite derivative rank condition makes the perturbed frequency map retain a positive-measure Diophantine preimage. In 59 this yields surface measure; in 60 the application with no normal variables and p replaced by p+q yields ambient volume. Smooth diffeomorphisms/generating-function assembly transfer that measure. This is a genuine proved measure strategy, subject to the constants and typographical concerns below, not a hidden appeal to analytic Rüssmann KAM.

There is useful quantitative information: Cor. 39 gives an inverse radius polynomial in γ; Lemma 49 records the weak-rank loss from the slow scale and localization; 59 claims a radius `C(a/b)^n1`; 60 uses polar actions of size ε and a near-identity Gε. These are the right patterns for a tube proof, **for smooth tori**. The actual coefficients, finite-order preparation estimates and all composed displacements must still be reconstructed. In particular the last flat-remainder sentence on p. 45 overstates what C∞ flatness implies; polynomial smallness is sufficient and should be proved instead. None of these observations proves an analytic upgrade.

## Route (c): quartic torsion and frozen P-G

P-G promises analytic Lagrangian Diophantine tori and a quantitative relative measure estimate in its literal product of oscillator discs, with constants uniform in its small radius and coupling. Once a physical quartic form has nonzero torsion on a fixed compact patch, it is an appropriate gate: choose a small positive secular radius r and patch first to fit the physical η-tube, and then choose `μ0 < r^6/(log(1/r))^c`. The radius may be fixed depending on η; it need not tend to zero with μ. Whole-torus containment is part of P-G's contract. Positive volume on a fundamental cell transports through the physical canonical chart.

This needs more than weak nondegeneracy: derive the quartic secular jet, put it in Birkhoff form (finite low-order nonresonance, or a justified symmetry-restricted normalization), show symmetric torsion with a uniform nonzero determinant on the patch, and verify P-G's neighborhood diffeomorphism, two-sided collar and joint analytic family hypotheses. ## Uniform tube and pair bounds: a concrete assembly contract

Fix the frozen data. Choose a smaller safe physical tube radius ζ depending on η and the positive numbers `min ai` and `min(i<j)(aj−ai)`. The phase-space Pi norm bounds each planar position error in Euclidean norm by `sqrt(2) ζ`. In particular choose ζ so that `2 sqrt(2) ζ` is smaller than half every axis gap and `sqrt(2) ζ` smaller than half every axis. This norm factor must not be omitted from blueprint-P/PLAN.md's current P6 sketch.

The compact reference torus and the circular-regular chart give an action neighborhood V near the prescribed axes and secular radius r such that the entire chart image `T^n × V × {|(ξ,η)|∞ < r}` lies in that ζ-tube (uniform continuity, or explicit bounded derivatives on a compact neighborhood). Use Part 10 to select a good weakly nondegenerate patch inside V, not necessarily at the original axes. Fix its closure and all analytic norms before the coupling. Part 11's quantified local theorem then gives the entire torus family inside this image for all smaller couplings. This yields `S ⊆ circularTube ζ ⊆ circularTube η`.
