### Concrete refutations of two estimate steps

For F02, put `ell=log(1/eps)`, `q=tau0+1`, `a=5 tau0+2` and `s2≈c ell^(-q)`. A holomorphic correction of the admissible size is

`g2(J,rho)=eps^3 ell^a ((J-J0)/s2) ((rho-rho0)/eps)`.

On the half-domain this has supremum `O(eps^3 ell^a)`. But `eps^(-1) d_rho g2=eps ell^a (J-J0)/s2`, whose first J derivative is `eps ell^a/s2`. The ratio to the claimed `C eps ell^a` is unbounded as eps tends to zero. This is a counterexample to the stated inference from the supplied sup bound, not a claim that this particular g2 occurs in the planetary construction. Increasing the fixed logarithmic exponent restores the needed convergence to zero.

For F03, a complex rotation with angle it has first column `(cosh t, i sinh t)`. It satisfies `R^T R=1`, but `|R e1|_infty=cosh t>1` for t>0. Thus the isometry estimate used off the real locus is false. Also, an analytic real rotation with angle `10 Lambda` has derivative of size 10 at the origin; shrinking its neighborhood cannot make that derivative ≤2. Compact complex-domain bounds, rather than real orthogonality, repair the argument.

## What I recomputed; refutation attempts that failed

- Period-one conversion: `J=2pi Lambda`, fast frequency `nu/(2pi)` and slow frequency `Omega/(2pi)` are compatible with the canonical chart. `Omega=2sigma` and its negative sign are preserved. No time multiplier is missing when the conformal chart and inverse Hamiltonian multiplier are both used.
- Fast cutoff: `exp(-2pi K1 sigma_F/6)=(eps M_F)^(nu1-1)` with nu1=3. Finite variation `K1 L s1≤gamma0 K1^(-tau0)/4` leaves the claimed divisor. S1 is an eps times finite-log smallness condition.
- Quadratic averaging constant: `(8/7)(1+2/e)=1.98372443696…<1.984`; multiplying by 48 gives less than 96; then multiplying by 4/3 gives less than 128, hence 130 is safe. This does not resolve the domain-generalization obligation.
- Slow averaging: `E2=O(eps^(5/2) ell^(2tau0+1))`, the B2 threshold is a constant times `eps^2 K2^(-tau0-1)`, and the ratio tends to zero. The quadratic correction has order `eps^3 ell^(5tau0+2)`. After rescaling, the final perturbation is `O(eps^(nu2+3/2) ell^(2tau0+1))`.
- The shell has positive real part componentwise. Its principal-root chart has the asserted O(sqrt(eps)) bound; no polar singularity lies in that chosen shell. The problem in F10 is proving the averaged extension through zero, not the shell chart itself.
- Amount: squared target scaling costs eps^2 and the J-to-r dilation costs at most eps^mu, giving eps^(mu+2). Taking the full set of directions can only improve the lower bound. No symmetry, extra slow rank or Birkhoff coefficient is needed for that implication.
- Budget: `nu2≥(mu+2)B0+2` leaves a positive power between the perturbation and the lower admissibility bound. `kappa_D nu2-(mu+2)a_D≥1` gives vanishing displacement. Dependence of intermediate constants on the already chosen nu2 causes no circularity after the fixed-envelope repair. The newly proved B0 above is adequate.
- Measure: tried to refute transport because the image of a parameter fundamental cell is not an ambient fundamental cell. This does not refute positivity: invariance, countable translates and the ambient cell partition supply it. The conformal determinant is eps^(2n), not eps or eps^(4n).
- Tube: real orthogonality, positive oscillator actions and the compact product-chart continuity argument work for arbitrary finite n. After using the correct displacement bounds, the final physical tube inclusion and finite-volume argument remain viable.
