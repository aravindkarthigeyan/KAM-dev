\section{Part 11: finite preparation and the properly degenerate local wrapper for P-FIXED}\label{bpP:p11}
\begingroup\sloppy

This part prepares the fixed-Sun Hamiltonian of Theorem~\ref{bpP:p0:fixed} (P-FIXED, general $n\ge2$) for the maximal-case analytic KAM theorem of Part~11a, and transports the resulting tori back to Cartesian coordinates. It follows CP09 \S2.3 (finite-order averaging, polar normalisation of the secular oscillators, the amount of non-degeneracy of the prepared frequency map, Lemma~8 and its polynomial budget) for the properly degenerate Hamiltonian $H=h(I)+\varepsilon f$ of CP09 Theorem~4 (here: $d=p=n$ fast angles and secular oscillators, so $N=d+p=2n$), with every quantitative gap of the source closed (reviews \texttt{p11-route-study.md}, \texttt{p11a-russmann-check.md}: R-CP-1, R-CP-2, A2, A4--A7). The route and its deviations in one paragraph.

\paragraph{Route.} (A) Part~7 (product chart), Part~8 (secular function), Part~9 (diagonalising rotation $\varrho$) put $H_{\rm fix}$ into the form $\tilde h(J)+\varepsilon F(\theta,J,x,y)$ with $F$ holomorphic on a strip and a complex action polydisc, $\theta\in\T^n$ the fast angles (period one), $(x,y)$ the rotated secular Cartesian pairs (Prop.~\ref{bpP:p11:holo-chart}). (B) A base point $J_0$ with Diophantine joint frequency $(\omega,\tilde\Omega)(J_0)$ is chosen by Part~11a-1 (Lemma~\ref{bpP:p11:select}; this replaces CP09 Lemma~3 and the erroneous set $K\cap D_{\gamma_0,\tau_0}$ of CP09 Lemma~3, errata item E-P-CP09-4 in \S\ref{bpP:p11:sec-notes}). (C) \emph{Fast averaging} (CP09 Lemma~4): Part~2's oscillator averaging removes the $\theta$-dependence up to $(\varepsilon M_F)^3$ on the action polydisc of radius $\asymp(\log\frac1\varepsilon)^{-(\tau_0+1)}$ (Prop.~\ref{bpP:p11:avg-fast}). (D) \emph{Polar normalisation and slow averaging}: on the shell $\abs{x_j}^2+\abs{y_j}^2\approx\varepsilon$ the oscillators are put in action--angle form $(\tilde\zeta,\tilde\rho)$, and a second two-scale averaging, now over all $2n$ angles with divisors $\alpha_1$ (fast) and $\varepsilon\alpha_2$ (slow), makes the remainder $O(\varepsilon^{\nu_2+3/2})$ for an arbitrary fixed $\nu_2$ (Lemma~\ref{bpP:p11:aniso-avg}, Prop.~\ref{bpP:p11:avg-slow}). \emph{This replaces CP09 Lemmas 5--7} (equilibrium shift, quadratic diagonalisation, finite Birkhoff normal form): the Birkhoff step is not needed for the Rüssmann gate because only the $J$-derivatives of the prepared frequency map enter the amount of non-degeneracy (Lemma~\ref{bpP:p11:amount}), and the exponentially small slow remainder replaces the $O(\abs{(u,v)}^{\nu_2+1})$ remainder. It also bypasses the errata A5 (polar domain) and A6 (Birkhoff formula) of the review; the price is that $\tilde\Omega(J_0)$ must be Diophantine, which Lemma~\ref{bpP:p11:select} provides for free (deviation D-11-1, \S\ref{bpP:p11:sec-notes}). (E) The \emph{prepared system} $\hat H_\varepsilon=N_\varepsilon+P_\varepsilon$ in rescaled actions $J=J_0+\varepsilon r$, $\tilde\rho=\varepsilon(2\pi+\rho')$ lives on a fixed compact $K_\varepsilon=[-\frac12,\frac12]^{2n}$ with $\vartheta=\frac14$ and $\varepsilon^*=\frac12$, so that \emph{every chart constant of Part~11a-5 is independent of $\varepsilon$ except the amount} $\beta_\varepsilon\asymp\varepsilon^{\bar\mu+2}$ (Prop.~\ref{bpP:p11:prepared}, Lemma~\ref{bpP:p11:amount}); this is the repaired form of CP09 Prop.~2 (R-CP-2) and of CP09 (42)--(47) (A4). (F) Theorem~\ref{bpP:p11:wrapper} applies the KAM input (Definition~\ref{bpP:p11:kam-input}) and transports the tori through the total chart to the tube $U_{\zeta_*}$.

\subsection{Conventions, external facts, and the KAM input}\label{bpP:p11:sec-conv}

\paragraph{Conventions.} Frozen conventions of Part~0: angles of period one, characters $\chi_k=e^{2\pi ik\cdot\theta}$, max norms $\abs\cdot_\infty$ on $\R^m,\C^m$ and induced operator norms. $D(c,r)=\{v\in\C^m:\abs{v-c}_\infty<r\}$ (complex polydisc), $\T_\sigma=\{\theta\in\C^m:\abs{\operatorname{Im}\theta}_\infty<\sigma\}$. For $V\subset\C^m$ open and a function $u$ on $\T_\sigma\times V$, $1$-periodic in the angles, the \emph{weighted norm} is that of \Nref{bpN:p4:norm},
\[\wn u V\sigma:=\sup_{x\in V}\sum_{k}\abs{\hat u_k(x)}\,e^{2\pi\abs k_1\sigma},\]
(Fourier expansion in the angles only; the remaining variables, including oscillator coordinates, play the r\^ole of the parameter $x$); $\sup\abs u\le\wn uV\sigma$ and $\wn uV\sigma\le\coth(\pi\sigma')^{m}\sup_{\T_{\sigma+\sigma'}\times V}\abs u$ (\Nref{bpN:p4:sup-weighted}). $c,c_1,\dots$ denote positive constants; a constant is always listed with the data it depends on. Period one is used for \emph{all} angles of the prepared problem. The period conversion is the canonical chart $\mathcal T(\theta,J,\zeta,\tilde\rho)=(2\pi\theta,J/2\pi,2\pi\zeta,\tilde\rho/2\pi)$ of Remark~\ref{bpP:p2:use5}(3) (a $1$-chart, $dJ\wedge d\theta=d\Lambda\wedge d\lambda$): the Hamiltonian is the same function, frequencies are divided by $2\pi$, and the frozen frequency is $w=\omega_{\rm CP}/2\pi$ (Lemma~\ref{bpP:p1:period-convert}).

\paragraph{External facts} (each used by name below): (e1) a real analytic function on an open set of $\R^m$ extends holomorphically to a complex neighbourhood of every compact subset (\Kref{bp:p1:holomorphic-analytic}); (e2) Cauchy estimates for holomorphic functions on polydiscs, $\abs{\partial^\alpha u}\le\alpha!\,\sup\abs u/\delta^{\abs\alpha}$ at points whose polydisc of radius $\delta$ lies in the domain (\Nref{bpN:p4:general-cauchy}); (e3) uniform continuity of a continuous function on a compact set; (e4) $\log\frac1\varepsilon\ge1$ and $\varepsilon^{a}(\log\frac1\varepsilon)^b\to0$ as $\varepsilon\to0$ for $a>0$; (e5) the Fourier average over an angle torus of a holomorphic periodic function is holomorphic in the remaining variables (\Nref{bpN:p4:coefficients}).

\begin{definition}[Quantitative KAM input, in the form in which it is used]\label{bpP:p11:kam-input}
This is a \emph{citation with exact interface}, not a hypothesis of any lemma of this part: it is \Pref{bpP:p11a:weak-kam-quantitative} together with \Pref{bpP:p11a:budget} (File~11a-5; forward references, not yet written). It is used only in the proof of Theorem~\ref{bpP:p11:wrapper}. Fix $N=2n$ and the squared convention $\rho=2$ of Definition~\ref{bpP:p11a:index-def}. A \emph{KAM datum} is $\mathfrak d=(\mu,\beta,K,\vartheta,s,M,C_1,\tau_D,\varepsilon^*)$ with $\mu\in\N_{\ge1}$, $\beta,\vartheta,s,M,C_1>0$, $K\subset\R^N$ compact, $\varepsilon^*\in(0,\operatorname{meas}K)$, $\tau_D>\max\{N\mu/2-1,\ N-1\}$. A Hamiltonian $H=h(r)+f(\theta,r)$, $(\theta,r)\in\T_s\times(K+4\vartheta)\subset\C^N\times\C^N$ (symplectic form $dr\wedge d\theta$), is \emph{$\mathfrak d$-admissible} if $h,f$ are holomorphic, $f$ is $1$-periodic in $\theta$, both are real on real points, and, with $\omega=\nabla h$,
\begin{enumerate}[label=(A\arabic*)]
\item $\beta_{2,\mu}(\omega,K)\ge\beta$ (Definition~\ref{bpP:p11a:index-def}); \item $\lvert D^kg_2[\omega](c,y)\rvert_{\mathrm{op}}\le M$ for $c\in S^{N-1}$, $1\le k\le\mu+1$, $y\in K_\vartheta$; \item $\abs\omega_\infty\le C_1$ and $\abs{D^2h}\le C_1$ on $K+3\vartheta$.
\end{enumerate}
\emph{Input.} There are $\gamma>0$ and $\varepsilon_{\rm adm}>0$, depending only on $\mathfrak d$, such that for every $\mathfrak d$-admissible $H$ with $\sup_{\T_s\times(K+4\vartheta)}\abs f\le\varepsilon_f\le\varepsilon_{\rm adm}$:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(K\arabic*)]
\item a compact $\mathcal H\subset K$ with $\operatorname{meas}\mathcal H\ge\operatorname{meas}K-\varepsilon^*$;
\item for $b\in\mathcal H$ a real analytic $\mathcal K_b:\R^N\to\R^N\times\R^N$ (positions, actions) with $\mathcal K_b(\theta+k)=\mathcal K_b(\theta)+(k,0)$ ($k\in\Z^N$) --- equivalently $u_b:=\mathcal K_b-(\mathrm{id},b)$ is $\Z^N$-periodic --- $D\mathcal K_b$ injective, $\mathcal K_b^*(dr\wedge d\theta)=0$, $\mathcal K_b(\theta)=\mathcal K_b(\theta')$ only if $\theta'-\theta\in\Z^N$, and a frequency $w_b\in\R^N$ that is $(\gamma,\tau_D)$-Diophantine with $s\mapsto\mathcal K_b(\theta+sw_b)$ having derivative $X_H(\mathcal K_b(\theta+tw_b))$ at every $t$;
\item $\sup_{b,\theta}\abs{u_b(\theta)}_\infty\le\Delta$ with $\Delta=\Delta(\varepsilon_f;\mathfrak d)\ge0$;
\item the evaluation map $(b,\theta)\mapsto\mathcal K_b(\theta)$ is injective on $\mathcal H\times[0,1)^N$, and $\operatorname{meas}\,\mathcal K(\mathcal H\times[0,1)^N)\ge2^{-2N}\operatorname{meas}\mathcal H$ (the set is Borel);
\item \emph{(budget)} if $(\mu,K,\vartheta,s,M,C_1,\tau_D,\varepsilon^*)$ are held fixed there are $c_*,c_D>0$, $B_0\ge1$, $\kappa_D>0$, $a_D\ge0$ (depending only on these) with $\varepsilon_{\rm adm}\ge c_*\beta^{B_0}$ and $\Delta\le c_D\,\varepsilon_f^{\kappa_D}\beta^{-a_D}$ for $\varepsilon_f\le\varepsilon_{\rm adm}$, uniformly in $\beta\in(0,1]$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{definition}

\subsection{Step A: the Hamiltonian in working coordinates}\label{bpP:p11:sec-A}

\paragraph{The fixed data and the patch.} $n\ge2$, $G,m_0>0$, $\kappa=Gm_0$, $m\in(0,\infty)^n$, $a\in\R^n$ with $0<a_1<\dots<a_n$, $\eta>0$ (Theorem~\ref{bpP:p0:fixed}; Lean index shift as in Part~12). $\zeta_*,d,D$ are those of Definition~\ref{bpP:p6:tube-constants}; $\Lambda^*_i=m_i\sqrt{\kappa a_i}$, $\rho_*=\rho_*(m,\kappa,a)$ of Proposition~\ref{bpP:p8:perturbation-analytic}. Let $\delta_*>0$ be the constant of Lemma~\ref{bpP:p12:forward-tube} for $\zeta=\zeta_*$ (the proof of that lemma uses $n=2$ only through the number of planets; Lemma~\ref{bpP:p11:forward-tube} restates it). Choose, by Theorem~\ref{bpP:p10:interface} (with the prescribed neighbourhood of $a$ so small that $\abs{\Lambda(a')-\Lambda^*}_\infty<\min\{\rho_*,\delta_*\}/8$), the patch $a_0,r_0,K_a$ and put $K_\Lambda=\Lambda(K_a)$, the index $\bar\mu=\mu_0\ge1$ and amount $\bar\beta>0$ of $\tilde\varpi=(\nu,\Omega)$ in the squared convention (Theorem~\ref{bpP:p10:interface}(d)), depending only on the data. In the period-one working variable $J=2\pi\Lambda$ put
\[\tilde h(J)=h(J/2\pi),\quad\omega(J)=\nabla\tilde h(J)=\tfrac1{2\pi}\nu(J/2\pi),\quad\tilde\Omega(J)=\tfrac1{2\pi}\Omega(J/2\pi),\quad\varpi_J=(\omega,\tilde\Omega):\ 2\pi B^\Lambda\to\R^{2n},\quad K_J=2\pi K_\Lambda,\]
with $\Omega_j=2\sigma_j$ (Remark~\ref{bpP:p9:dict}) and $\tilde\Omega_j<0$.

\begin{lemma}[Index and amount in the working variable]\label{bpP:p11:norm-dict}
$\varpi_J$ is real analytic and R-nondegenerate on $2\pi B^\Lambda$, and $\beta_{2,\bar\mu}(\varpi_J,K_J)\ge(2\pi)^{-2-\bar\mu}\bar\beta=:\beta_J>0$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
\proofstep{Constant factor and dilation}\label{stepP:p11:nd} $\varpi_J(J)=(2\pi)^{-1}\tilde\varpi(J/2\pi)$. Hence $g_2[\varpi_J](c,J)=(2\pi)^{-2}g_2[\tilde\varpi](c,J/2\pi)$ (Definition~\ref{bpP:p11a:index-def}) and $D_J^\nu=(2\pi)^{-\nu}D_\Lambda^\nu$, so $\max_{\nu\le\bar\mu}\lvert D^\nu_Jg_2[\varpi_J](c,J)\rvert\ge(2\pi)^{-2-\bar\mu}\max_{\nu\le\bar\mu}\lvert D^\nu_\Lambda g_2[\tilde\varpi](c,\Lambda)\rvert\ge(2\pi)^{-2-\bar\mu}\bar\beta$ for $c\in S^{2n-1}$, $J\in K_J$ (Theorem~\ref{bpP:p10:interface}(d)). R-nondegeneracy is invariant under a positive diagonal rescaling and an affine change of the variable (Theorem~\ref{bpP:p10:rnd}(c), Remark~\ref{bpP:p10:scaling}).
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Base point]\label{bpP:p11:select}
Put $\tau_0=\max\{n\bar\mu,\,2n\}$ and $\varepsilon_0^*=\tfrac12\operatorname{meas}K_J$. There are $\gamma_0\in(0,1]$ and $J_0\in\operatorname{int}K_J$, depending only on the data, with $\abs{k\cdot\varpi_J(J_0)}\ge\gamma_0\abs k_1^{-\tau_0}$ for all $k\in\Z^{2n}\setminus\{0\}$. In particular $\omega(J_0)$ and $\tilde\Omega(J_0)$ are $(\gamma_0,\tau_0)$-Diophantine in $\Z^n$ (Definition~\ref{bpP:p0:diophantine}).
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
\proofstep{Hypotheses of the measure estimate}\label{stepP:p11:sel-hyp} Apply Corollary~\ref{bpP:p11a:power-law} with $N=n$, $m=2n$, $\rho=2$, $\mu=\bar\mu$, $\omega=\varpi_J$, $\tilde\omega_k=\varpi_J$ for all $k$, the compact set $K_J$ (nonempty, positive measure, $d=\operatorname{diam}_\infty K_J>0$), $\vartheta>0$ so small that $(K_J)_\vartheta\subset 2\pi B^\Lambda$, $\beta=\beta_J$ (Lemma~\ref{bpP:p11:norm-dict}) and $M=\max\{\beta_J,M_1\}$ of Lemma~\ref{bpP:p11a:M-bound}. (D1) is Lemma~\ref{bpP:p11:norm-dict}, (D2) is Lemma~\ref{bpP:p11a:M-bound}, (D3) is trivial because $\tilde\omega_k=\omega$. The exponent condition is $\tau_0>m\mu/\rho-1=n\bar\mu-1$.
\proofstep{Choice of $\gamma_0$}\label{stepP:p11:sel-gam} By the Corollary $\operatorname{meas}(K_J\setminus H_\gamma)\le C_{\rm Dio}\gamma^{2/\bar\mu}$ for $\gamma\le\gamma_*(\varepsilon^*_0)$; take $\gamma_0=\min\{1,\gamma_*(\varepsilon_0^*)\}$. Then $\operatorname{meas}H_{\gamma_0}\ge\operatorname{meas}K_J-\varepsilon_0^*>0$. Here $H_{\gamma_0}=\{b\in K_J:\abs{k\cdot\varpi_J(b)}\ge\gamma_0\abs k_1^{-\tau_0}\ \forall k\ne0\}$ since the map does not depend on $k$.
\proofstep{Interior point}\label{stepP:p11:sel-int} $K_J\setminus\operatorname{int}K_J$ is the boundary of a cube (the map $a\mapsto2\pi\Lambda(a)$ acts coordinatewise and is increasing, so it maps the cube $K_a$ to a cube), a null set. Hence $H_{\gamma_0}\cap\operatorname{int}K_J\neq\emptyset$; take $J_0$ in it. The restriction to $k=(k_1,0)$ and $k=(0,k_2)$ is the last sentence.
\end{proof}
This replaces CP09 Lemma 3 (\texttt{p11a-russmann-check}, A7): the object is the set of \emph{parameters} $b$ with $\varpi_J(b)\in\mathcal D_{\gamma_0,\tau_0}$, not $K\cap\mathcal D$; the exponent of CP09 ($\tau_0\ge d\mu_0$) is replaced by the weaker $\tau_0>n\bar\mu-1$ proved in File~11a-1 (deviation D5 there).

\begin{proposition}[Holomorphic secular form near the base point]\label{bpP:p11:holo-chart}
Let $J_0,\gamma_0,\tau_0$ be as in Lemma~\ref{bpP:p11:select}. There are $r_C\in(0,1]$, $\sigma_F\in(0,\frac1{4}]$, $r_z\in(0,1]$, $M_F\ge1$, $L\ge1$ and a function $F$, all depending only on the data (and $J_0$), such that, with $\mathcal J=D(J_0,2r_C)\subset\C^n$ and $z=(x,y)\in\C^{2n}$ (position $x$, momentum $y$):
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\alph*)]
\item $F$ is holomorphic on $\T_{2\sigma_F}\times\mathcal J\times B_{r_z}$ ($B_{r_z}=D(0,r_z)\subset\C^{2n}$), $1$-periodic in $\theta$, real on real points, and $\abs F\le M_F/\coth(\pi\sigma_F)^n$ there; hence $\wn F{\mathcal J\times B_{r_z}}{\sigma_F}\le M_F$ in the sense of \S\ref{bpP:p11:sec-conv} with $z$ as a parameter. Moreover $\omega,\tilde\Omega$ are holomorphic on $\mathcal J$ and $\lvert D^j\varpi_J\rvert_{\mathrm{op}}\le L$ for $0\le j\le\bar\mu+1$ there (so $\lvert D\omega\rvert,\lvert D\tilde\Omega\rvert,\lvert\varpi_J\rvert\le L$).
\item (\emph{identity}) $\Psi_c:=\Psi^{(n)}\circ\Phi_\varrho^{-1}\circ\mathcal T_1$, with $\mathcal T_1(\theta,J,x,y)=(2\pi\theta,J/2\pi,x,y)$ and $\varrho:D(\Lambda_0,2r_C/2\pi)\to SO(n)$, $\Lambda_0=J_0/2\pi$, the analytic rotation of Proposition~\ref{bpP:p9:local-analytic} (diagonalising $Q_h$), is a symplectic $1$-chart on $\mathcal O_c=\{(\theta,J,z)\in\R^{2n}\times\R^{2n}:J\in\mathcal J\cap\R^n,\ \abs z_\infty<r_z\}$ with values in the collision-free domain, and for all real $\varepsilon$ and $(\theta,J,z)\in\mathcal O_c$: $H_{\rm fix}\bigl(\Psi_c(\theta,J,z)\bigr)=\tilde h(J)+\varepsilon F(\theta,J,z)$.
\item (\emph{lattice}) $\Psi_c$ satisfies (Q1)--(Q2) of Theorem~\ref{bpP:p1:torus-transport} for the lattice $\iota_k=((k,0),0)$ shifting $\theta$ and $\sigma_k=0$ (the secular positions $x$ are not shifted; the secular lattice enters after the polar chart, Lemma~\ref{bpP:p11:total-chart}).
\item (\emph{mean}) $F_0:=\hat F_0$ (the Fourier average over $\theta$) is holomorphic on $\mathcal J\times B_{r_z}$ and
\[F_0(J,z)=C(J)+\sum_{j=1}^n\Omega_j(J)\tfrac12(x_j^2+y_j^2)+R_4(J,z),\qquad\abs{R_4(J,z)}\le M_F\bigl(\abs z_\infty/r_z\bigr)^4,\]
with $C(J)=C_0(a(J/2\pi))$, $\Omega_j(J)=2\pi\tilde\Omega_j(J)$ and real analytic $C$. Both models would give this form (Corollary~\ref{bpP:p8:same-jets}); only $F_0$ of the fixed-Sun model is used.
\end{enumerate}
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
\proofstep{Rotation and its extension}\label{stepP:p11:hc-rot} $Q_h$ has simple spectrum on the ball $\mathcal W\ni\Lambda_0$ (Theorem~\ref{bpP:p10:interface}(a), $J_0\in K_J$), so $\sigma_j,\varrho$ are real analytic on a ball $D_\R(\Lambda_0,t_1)$ (Proposition~\ref{bpP:p9:local-analytic}); extend holomorphically to a complex polydisc (e1), shrinking so that $\varrho^\top\varrho=1$ (identity theorem), $\varrho$ and $\partial\varrho$ are bounded by $2$, and $\varrho$ is real at real points. This fixes $r_C$ (with $2r_C\le\pi t_1$).
\proofstep{The map $\Phi_\varrho^{-1}$ on the complex domain}\label{stepP:p11:hc-lift} By Proposition~\ref{bpP:p9:lift}, $\Phi_\varrho^{-1}(\tilde\lambda,\Lambda,\tilde\xi,\tilde\eta)=(\tilde\lambda+H_\varrho,\Lambda,\varrho\tilde\xi,\varrho\tilde\eta)$ with $H_{\varrho,l}=\tilde\eta^\top(\varrho^\top\partial_{\Lambda_l}\varrho)\tilde\xi$, a holomorphic function with $\abs{H_\varrho}_\infty\le c\,n^2\abs z_\infty^2$ for $\abs z_\infty<1$ (bilinear in $z$, entries bounded by $4$). For $\abs z_\infty<r_z$ with $r_z\le\rho_*/(4\sqrt n)$ one has $\abs{\varrho\tilde\xi}_\infty\le\abs{\tilde\xi}_2\le\sqrt n\abs{\tilde\xi}_\infty<\rho_*$.
\proofstep{Strip extension of $\hat f_{\rm fix}$}\label{stepP:p11:hc-strip} Part~8 proves the holomorphy of $\hat f_{\rm fix}$ in $w'=(\xi,\Lambda,\eta)\in\mathcal N(\rho_*)$ only for real $\lambda$ (Proposition~\ref{bpP:p8:perturbation-analytic}(b)). Here the angular strip is needed. By Proposition~\ref{bpP:p7:holomorphic} $s_{ij}(\lambda,w')$ is holomorphic on $\{\abs{\operatorname{Im}\lambda}<\frac14\}\times\mathcal N(\rho_0)$ and $2\pi$-periodic; by Proposition~\ref{bpP:p8:perturbation-analytic}(a) $\operatorname{Re}s_{ij}\ge\frac12(a_j-a_i)^2$ at real $\lambda$ and $w'\in\mathcal N(\rho_*)$ (the $a_i$ here are the ordered axes of the data, $a_i^*$ of Part~8). By (e3) applied to $[0,2\pi]^n\times\overline{\mathcal N(\rho_*/2)}$ (and the $2\pi$-periodicity in $\operatorname{Re}\lambda$) there is $\sigma'\in(0,\frac14]$ with $\operatorname{Re}s_{ij}\ge\frac14(a_j-a_i)^2$ for $\abs{\operatorname{Im}\lambda}_\infty\le2\pi\sigma'$ (a strip of period-one width $\sigma'$ in $\theta=\lambda/2\pi$) and $w'\in\mathcal N(\rho_*/2)$. On this set $\hat f_{\rm fix}=-G\sum_{i<j}m_im_js_{ij}^{-1/2}$ (principal root) is holomorphic and bounded by $c(m,G,a)$.
\proofstep{Definition of $F$ and (a)}\label{stepP:p11:hc-F} $F:=\hat f_{\rm fix}\circ\Phi_\varrho^{-1}\circ\mathcal T_1$ is holomorphic where the composition is defined: $\theta\in\T_{2\sigma_F}$ with $2\sigma_F\le\sigma'/2$, the shift $\abs{\operatorname{Im}H_\varrho}\le cn^2r_z^2<\pi\sigma'/2$ after shrinking $r_z$, $\mathcal J\subset D(J_0,2r_C)$ so $\Lambda\in D(\Lambda_0,r_C/\pi)\subset\mathcal N$-range. $F$ is $1$-periodic in $\theta$ ($\hat f_{\rm fix}$ is $2\pi$-periodic in $\lambda$ and $H_\varrho$ does not depend on $\tilde\lambda$), real at real points ($\varrho$, $\hat f$ real). Its sup is some $M_\infty$; put $M_F=\max\{1,\coth(\pi\sigma_F)^nM_\infty\}$ and use \Nref{bpN:p4:sup-weighted}(b) with the extra strip $\sigma_F$. $L$ bounds $\varpi_J$ and its derivatives up to order $\bar\mu+1$ on $\mathcal J$ (holomorphic on a neighbourhood of $\overline{\mathcal J}$ after shrinking $r_C$; Cauchy, (e2)).
\proofstep{(b) Identity and chart}\label{stepP:p11:hc-id} For real points the composition $\Psi^{(n)}\circ\Phi_\varrho^{-1}\circ\mathcal T_1$ lies in $\Psi^{(n)}(\mathcal N_\R(\rho_*)\times\R^n)\subset W$ (Proposition~\ref{bpP:p8:perturbation-analytic}(a)); Theorem~\ref{bpP:p7:product-chart}(c) gives $H_{\rm fix}\circ\Psi^{(n)}=h(\Lambda)+\varepsilon\hat f_{\rm fix}$ and $\Phi_\varrho$ preserves $\Lambda$ (Proposition~\ref{bpP:p9:lift}), $\mathcal T_1$ maps $J\mapsto\Lambda=J/2\pi$: $h(\Lambda)=\tilde h(J)$. Symplecticity: $\Psi^{(n)}$ (Theorem~\ref{bpP:p7:product-chart}(a)), $\Phi_\varrho^{-1}$ (Proposition~\ref{bpP:p9:lift}), $\mathcal T_1$ ($d\Lambda\wedge d\lambda=dJ\wedge d\theta$), Remark~\ref{bpP:p1:typical}(3); the chart is real analytic because $F$ and the maps are (Lemma~\ref{bpP:p1:hol-chart}).
\proofstep{(c) Lattice}\label{stepP:p11:hc-lat} $\mathcal T_1(\theta+k,\cdot)=\mathcal T_1(\theta,\cdot)+(2\pi k,0,0,0)$, $\Phi_\varrho^{-1}$ commutes with $\tilde\lambda$-shifts, $\Psi^{(n)}$ is invariant under $2\pi\Z^n$-shifts of $\lambda$ with injectivity modulo them (Theorem~\ref{bpP:p7:product-chart}(a)); $\mathcal T_1$ and $\Phi_\varrho$ are injective. This is Theorem~\ref{bpP:p9:diagonalize}(d) with $\mathcal T_1$.
\proofstep{(d) Mean}\label{stepP:p11:hc-mean} The $\theta$-average of $F$ is, by (e5) and Theorem~\ref{bpP:p9:diagonalize}(b), $S_{\rm fix}(\Lambda,\varrho x,\varrho y)=C_0+\sum_j\Omega_j\tilde R_j+O(\abs{(x,y)}^4)$ with $\tilde R_j=\frac12(x_j^2+y_j^2)$; the bound with explicit constant follows from Cauchy on $B_{r_z}$ after enlarging $M_F$: the function $R_4=F_0-C-\sum\Omega_j\tilde R_j$ is holomorphic with vanishing jet to order $3$ at $z=0$ (Corollary~\ref{bpP:p8:evenness}: even in $(\xi,\eta)$, no linear term, quadratic part as stated), hence $\abs{R_4}\le\sup\abs{R_4}(\abs z_\infty/r_z)^4$ (Schwarz lemma on polydiscs applied on the lines $z=tz_0$).
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Total chart and lattice compatibility]\label{bpP:p11:total-chart}
Let $\Theta^{(1)}(\zeta,\tilde\rho)=\sqrt{\tilde\rho/\pi}\,(\sin2\pi\zeta,\cos2\pi\zeta)$ componentwise for $\tilde\rho_j>0$ (so $x_j=\sqrt{\tilde\rho_j/\pi}\sin2\pi\zeta_j$, $y_j=\sqrt{\tilde\rho_j/\pi}\cos2\pi\zeta_j$, $\frac12(x_j^2+y_j^2)=\tilde\rho_j/2\pi$). \[K_\varepsilon=[-\tfrac12,\tfrac12]^{2n},\ \ \vartheta=\tfrac14,\ \ \varepsilon^*=\tfrac12,\ \ s=s_*:=\sigma_3/6=\sigma_F/72,\ \ N=2n,\ \ \mu=\bar\mu,\ \ \tau_D=\max\{n\bar\mu,2n\},\]
so $\operatorname{meas}K_\varepsilon=1$, $d=1$, $K_\varepsilon+4\vartheta\subset D(0,\frac32)^{2n}\subset\{\abs r<2,\ \abs{\rho'}<\frac\pi2\}$. Frequency map of $N_\varepsilon$: $\hat\omega_\varepsilon=\nabla_{(r,\rho')}N_\varepsilon=(\partial_Jh,\ \partial_{\tilde\rho}h)(J_0+\varepsilon r,\varepsilon(2\pi\mathbf1+\rho'))$ with $h=h_0+g_2$. It is convenient to write $\hat\omega_\varepsilon=(\hat\omega^{(1)},\varepsilon\hat\omega^{(2)})$ with
\[\hat\omega^{(1)}=\omega+\varepsilon\partial_J\hat G_0+\partial_Jg_2,\qquad\hat\omega^{(2)}=\tilde\Omega+\partial_{\tilde\rho}\hat Z+\varepsilon^{-1}\partial_{\tilde\rho}g_2\qquad\text{(arguments as above).}\]

\begin{proposition}[The prepared system]\label{bpP:p11:prepared}
For $0<\varepsilon\le\varepsilon_2$ the functions $N_\varepsilon,P_\varepsilon$ are holomorphic on $\mathcal E$, $1$-periodic in the angles, real on real points, $N_\varepsilon$ depends on $(r,\rho')$ only, and $H_2\circ A_\varepsilon=\varepsilon\hat H_\varepsilon$. Moreover, with $c_3=c_3(\text{data},\nu_2)$:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\alph*)]
\item $\sup_{\mathcal E}\abs{P_\varepsilon}\le c_3\,\varepsilon^{\nu_2+3/2}(\log\frac1\varepsilon)^{2\tau_0+1}=:\varepsilon_f(\varepsilon)$;
\item $\abs{\hat\omega_\varepsilon}_\infty\le C_1:=2L+1$ and $\abs{D^2N_\varepsilon}_\infty\le C_1$ on $K_\varepsilon+3\vartheta$;
\item for $\abs\alpha\le\bar\mu+1$ with $\alpha$ a derivative in $J$ only: $\sup_{J\in D(J_0,s_1/4)}\abs{\partial_J^\alpha(\hat\omega^{(i)}-\varpi^{(i)})}\le\theta_\varepsilon:=c_3\,\varepsilon(\log\frac1\varepsilon)^{5\tau_0+2}$ ($i=1,2$, derivative norm as in Definition~\ref{bpP:p11a:index-def} up to a factor $c(n,\bar\mu)$; $\varpi^{(1)}=\omega$, $\varpi^{(2)}=\tilde\Omega$), uniformly in $\tilde\rho\in\varepsilon(2\pi\mathbf1+D(0,\frac\pi2))$;
\item for the full derivatives of order $k\le\bar\mu+1$ in $(r,\rho')$ on $K_\varepsilon+\vartheta$: $\lvert D^kg_2[\hat\omega_\varepsilon](c,y)\rvert_{\mathrm{op}}\le M_*$ ($c\in S^{2n-1}$), with $M_*=2^{\bar\mu+1}(2L+1)^2$ independent of $\varepsilon$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
\proofstep{Holomorphy, reality, periodicity}\label{stepP:p11:pr-basic} $\Phi^1_\varepsilon$, $\hat\Theta$, $\Psi_2$ are real holomorphic with the lattice properties, $H_2\circ A_\varepsilon=H\circ(\Phi^1\circ\hat\Theta\circ\Psi_2\circ A_\varepsilon)$; the domain inclusions are those of Propositions~\ref{bpP:p11:avg-fast}, \ref{bpP:p11:avg-slow}: $\Psi_2$ is defined on $\T_{\sigma_3/6}\times\tfrac12V_2\supset A_\varepsilon(\mathcal E)$ and maps into $\T_{\sigma_3}\times V_2$, on which $\hat\Theta$ is defined and takes values in $\mathcal D_1'$ ($\abs\Pi\le r_z/2$, $J\in D(J_0,s_1)$, strip $\sigma_3\le\sigma_F/6$). $N_\varepsilon$ is $\tfrac1\varepsilon(h_0+g_2)\circ A_\varepsilon$, a function of $(r,\rho')$; $P_\varepsilon=\varepsilon^{-1}f_3\circ A_\varepsilon$.
\proofstep{(a)}\label{stepP:p11:pr-a} $\sup\abs{f_3}\le\lVert f_3\rVert\le\varepsilon^{\nu_2}E_2$ (\Nref{bpN:p4:sup-weighted}(a)), divide by $\varepsilon$.
\proofstep{(b)}\label{stepP:p11:pr-b} Write $h=h_0+g_2$. $\partial_rN_\varepsilon=\partial_Jh=\omega+\varepsilon\partial_J\hat G_0+\partial_Jg_2$ and $\partial_{\rho'}N_\varepsilon=\partial_{\tilde\rho}h=\varepsilon(\tilde\Omega+\partial_{\tilde\rho}\hat Z)+\partial_{\tilde\rho}g_2$; by (e2) with radii $s_1/4$ (in $J$) and $\pi\varepsilon/4$ resp.\ $\pi r_z^2/16$ (in $\tilde\rho$), Proposition~\ref{bpP:p11:avg-slow}(a),(c) and $\lvert\hat G_0\rvert\le c$ these are $\le L+o(1)$ and $o(1)$. The Hessian: $\partial_r^2N_\varepsilon=\varepsilon\partial_J^2h$, $\partial_r\partial_{\rho'}N_\varepsilon=\varepsilon\partial_J\partial_{\tilde\rho}h$, $\partial_{\rho'}^2N_\varepsilon=\varepsilon\partial_{\tilde\rho}^2h$, with $\partial_{\tilde\rho}^2h=\varepsilon\partial^2\hat Z+\partial^2_{\tilde\rho}g_2$, $\partial_{\tilde\rho}^2g_2=O(\varepsilon^{3}\varepsilon^{-2}\log^c)$; all entries are $o(1)$ as $\varepsilon\to0$ (polynomial in $\log\frac1\varepsilon$ against a positive power of $\varepsilon$). So $\lvert\hat\omega_\varepsilon\rvert_\infty\le C_1=2L+1$ and $\lvert D^2N_\varepsilon\rvert\le C_1$ for $\varepsilon\le\varepsilon_3$.
\proofstep{(c)}\label{stepP:p11:pr-c} $\hat\omega^{(1)}-\omega=\varepsilon\partial_J\hat G_0+\partial_Jg_2$: $\partial_J^\alpha$ of the first term is $\le\varepsilon\alpha!\,M_F(2/s_1)^{\abs\alpha+1}$ by (e2) (the sup of $\hat G_0$ is $\le c M_F$ on $D(J_0,s_1/2)$) and of the second is $\le\abs\alpha!\,\sup\abs{g_2}(4/s_2)^{\abs\alpha+1}\le c\varepsilon^3\log^{c}$. $\hat\omega^{(2)}-\tilde\Omega=\partial_{\tilde\rho}\hat Z+\varepsilon^{-1}\partial_{\tilde\rho}g_2$: the first is $\le c_2(\varepsilon+\eta_1)$ by Proposition~\ref{bpP:p11:avg-slow}(a) (all $J$-derivatives included), the second is bounded by Cauchy in $\tilde\rho$ at radius $\pi\varepsilon/4$ and in $J$ at radius $s_2/4$: $\varepsilon^{-1}\cdot\frac{4}{\pi\varepsilon}\sup\abs{g_2}(4/s_2)^{\abs\alpha}\alpha!\le c\varepsilon\log^c\frac1\varepsilon$. \emph{Only $J$-derivatives enter.}
\proofstep{(d)}\label{stepP:p11:pr-d} By Lemma~\ref{bpP:p11a:M-bound}, $\lvert D^kg_2[\hat\omega_\varepsilon]\rvert_{\mathrm{op}}\le2^kM_1^2$ with $M_1=\lVert\hat\omega_\varepsilon\rVert^{\bar\mu+1}_{{\rm op},K_\varepsilon+\vartheta}$. The main terms of $\hat\omega_\varepsilon$ are $(\omega(J_0+\varepsilon r),\ \varepsilon\tilde\Omega(J_0+\varepsilon r))$: independent of $\rho'$, and each $r$-derivative of order $j\ge1$ carries a factor $\varepsilon^j$, so their norms in $C^{\bar\mu+1}$ are $\le L$. The remaining terms are $\varepsilon\partial_J\hat G_0+\partial_Jg_2$ and $\varepsilon\partial_{\tilde\rho}\hat Z+\partial_{\tilde\rho}g_2$: a derivative of order $j=j_J+j_\rho$ in $(r,\rho')$ is $\varepsilon^{j}$ times a derivative in $(J,\tilde\rho)$, bounded by (e2) with radii $s_1/4$, resp.\ $\pi r_z^2/16$ for $\hat Z$ and $\pi\varepsilon/4$ for $g_2$ (where $\sup\lvert g_2\rvert\le c\varepsilon^3\log^c$ against the factor $\varepsilon^{j_\rho}\varepsilon^{-j_\rho-1}$); every such term is $o(1)$. Hence $M_1\le2L+1$ for $\varepsilon\le\varepsilon_3$ and $M_*:=2^{\bar\mu+1}(2L+1)^2$ works.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Directional stability and target scaling; R-CP-2]\label{bpP:p11:amount}
Let $\varepsilon\le\varepsilon_3\le\varepsilon_2$ be such that $2^{\bar\mu}\theta_\varepsilon(2L+\theta_\varepsilon)\le\tfrac12\beta_J$ with $\theta_\varepsilon$ of Proposition~\ref{bpP:p11:prepared}(c) and $\beta_J$ of Lemma~\ref{bpP:p11:norm-dict}. Then, in the sense of Definition~\ref{bpP:p11a:index-def} with $\rho=2$, $m=N=2n$,
\[\beta_{2,\bar\mu}(\hat\omega_\varepsilon,K_\varepsilon)\ \ge\ \beta_\varepsilon:=\tfrac12\,\varepsilon^{\bar\mu+2}\beta_J>0 .\]
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
\proofstep{Normalisation of the second block}\label{stepP:p11:am-norm} Put $\hat\omega_\varepsilon=\mathrm{diag}(\mathbf1_n,\varepsilon\mathbf1_n)\,\check\omega_\varepsilon$, $\check\omega_\varepsilon=(\hat\omega^{(1)},\hat\omega^{(2)})$ (Prop.~\ref{bpP:p11:prepared}(c) notation). For $D=\operatorname{diag}(1,\varepsilon)$ and $c\in S^{2n-1}$: $\langle c,D\check\omega\rangle=\langle Dc,\check\omega\rangle=\abs{Dc}\langle\hat c,\check\omega\rangle$ with $\hat c=Dc/\abs{Dc}\in S^{2n-1}$, $\varepsilon\le\abs{Dc}\le1$. So $g_2[D\check\omega](c,\cdot)=\abs{Dc}^2g_2[\check\omega](\hat c,\cdot)$ and $\beta_{2,\mu}(D\check\omega,K)\ge\varepsilon^2\beta_{2,\mu}(\check\omega,K)$.
\proofstep{Restriction to $r$-directions}\label{stepP:p11:am-dir} Fix $\rho'_1\in[-\frac12,\frac12]^n$, $\tilde\rho_1=\varepsilon(2\pi\mathbf1+\rho'_1)$, and on $V=D_\R(J_0,s_1/4)\subset\R^n$ consider $\psi_\varepsilon(J)=\check\omega_\varepsilon$ evaluated at $(J,\tilde\rho_1)$ and $\psi(J)=\varpi_J(J)$, both maps $V\to\R^{2n}$ in $C^{\bar\mu}$. By Proposition~\ref{bpP:p11:prepared}(c), $\theta:=\lVert\psi_\varepsilon-\psi\rVert^{\bar\mu}_{\mathrm{dg},A}\le\theta_\varepsilon$ ($A\supset$ the compact $\{J_0+\varepsilon r:\abs r\le\frac12\}$ for $\varepsilon\le s_1/2$), and $M_0:=\lVert\psi\rVert^{\bar\mu}_{\mathrm{dg},A}\le c(n,\bar\mu)L=:L_1$ (Proposition~\ref{bpP:p11:holo-chart}(a)); we read $L$ in the hypothesis of the lemma as $L_1$. Lemma~\ref{bpP:p11a:stability} (with $N=n$, $m=2n$, $\rho=2$) gives, since $\beta_{2,\bar\mu}(\psi,A)\ge\beta_J$ (Lemma~\ref{bpP:p11:norm-dict}, $A\subset K_J$ for small $\varepsilon$ because $J_0\in\operatorname{int}K_J$) and $2^{\bar\mu}\theta(2M_0+\theta)\le\frac12\beta_J$: $\beta_{2,\bar\mu}(\psi_\varepsilon,A)\ge\frac12\beta_J$, where the $n$-dimensional amount is the minimum over $(c,J)$ of $\max_\nu\max_{a\in S^{n-1}}\lvert D_J^\nu g_2[\psi_\varepsilon](c,J)(a^\nu)\rvert$.
\proofstep{Rescaling of the actions}\label{stepP:p11:am-scale} The $(r,\rho')$-derivative of $g_2[\check\omega_\varepsilon\text{ in rescaled variables}](c,\cdot)$ in a direction $(a,0)$, $a\in S^{n-1}$, at $(r,\rho'_1)$ equals $\varepsilon^\nu D^\nu_Jg_2[\psi_\varepsilon](c,J)(a^\nu)$ with $J=J_0+\varepsilon r$ (chain rule for the dilation, Lemma~\ref{bpP:p11a:chart-scaling}); the full tensor norm (Definition~\ref{bpP:p11a:index-def}, maximum over all unit directions $a\in S^{2n-1}$) is at least its value on directions $(a,0)$ (note $\abs{(a,0)}_2=1$). Therefore $\beta_{2,\bar\mu}(\check\omega_\varepsilon,K_\varepsilon)\ge\min\{1,\varepsilon^{\bar\mu}\}\tfrac12\beta_J=\varepsilon^{\bar\mu}\tfrac12\beta_J$ (as $\max_\nu\varepsilon^\nu x_\nu\ge\varepsilon^{\bar\mu}\max_\nu x_\nu$, $\varepsilon<1$); the dependence on $\rho'$ is only through the frozen parameter, and the minimum over $\rho'_1$ is trivial because the bound is uniform. Combine with Step~\ref{stepP:p11:am-norm}: $\beta_{2,\bar\mu}(\hat\omega_\varepsilon,K_\varepsilon)\ge\varepsilon^{2}\cdot\varepsilon^{\bar\mu}\tfrac12\beta_J=\beta_\varepsilon$. Euclidean norms on spheres as in Definition~\ref{bpP:p11a:index-def}.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[R-CP-2 and what is not claimed]\label{bpP:p11:r-cp-2}
CP09 p.~17 asserts $\beta_\varepsilon\ge\varepsilon^{\bar\mu}\bar\beta/2$ from $\Psi_\varepsilon=\Psi_0+O(\varepsilon)$ in $C^0$ of the \emph{rescaled} variable; that is insufficient (the amount is $\asymp\varepsilon^{\bar\mu}$, a $C^0$-perturbation of size $\varepsilon$ is larger). Here the perturbation is measured in $C^{\bar\mu}$ in the \emph{unscaled} action $J$ (Proposition~\ref{bpP:p11:prepared}(c)), where it is $O(\varepsilon\log^{5\tau_0+2}\frac1\varepsilon)$ against the unscaled amount $\beta_J$; the rescaling then multiplies both by the same $\varepsilon^\nu$ (Lemma~\ref{bpP:p11:amount}). The $\rho'$-dependence of $N_\varepsilon$ is not controlled in $C^{\bar\mu}$ at all (only through Proposition~\ref{bpP:p11:prepared}(d), a bound), and is not needed: the amount in the full sense is bounded below by the amount in the $r$-directions. Also corrected: CP09 (17), (28) and Prop.~2 carry a spurious factor $\frac12$ in front of $\hat\Omega\cdot(\rho_0+\varepsilon\rho)$ (with $z_j=\sqrt{2(\rho_{0j}+\rho_j)}e^{-i\zeta_j}$ one has $\frac12(u_j^2+v_j^2)=\rho_{0j}+\rho_j$), which changes only the constant in the amount.
\end{remark}

\subsection{Step F: the local wrapper and the transport of the tori}\label{bpP:p11:sec-F}

\begin{lemma}[Forward tube for $n$ planets]\label{bpP:p11:forward-tube}
Let $a_i>0$, $\Lambda_i^*=m_i\sqrt{\kappa a_i}$, $\zeta>0$. There is $\delta_\zeta\in(0,\frac14\min_i\Lambda_i^*]$, depending only on $(G,m_0,m,a,\zeta)$, such that every $w=((\lambda,\xi),(\Lambda,\eta))\in\R^{4n}$ with $\abs{\Lambda_i-\Lambda_i^*}\le\delta_\zeta$ and $\Gamma_i(w)\le\delta_\zeta$ for all $i$ lies in $\mathcal O_n$ and satisfies $\abs{\Psi^{(n)}(w)-\operatorname{circ}(\lambda/2\pi)}_\infty<\zeta$; in particular $\Psi^{(n)}(w)\in U_\zeta$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Steps~\ref{stepP:p12:ft-cpt}--\ref{stepP:p12:ft-cmp} of Lemma~\ref{bpP:p12:forward-tube} with $2$ replaced by $n$ in the index ranges and $\R^8$ by $\R^{4n}$; nothing else changes (compact set $Q_0$, uniform continuity (e3) of $\Psi^{(n)}$, $\Psi^{(n)}(w_0)=\operatorname{circ}(\lambda/2\pi)$ by Lemma~\ref{bpP:p7:tube}).
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Local wrapper for P-FIXED: tori in the tube]\label{bpP:p11:wrapper}
Let $n\ge2$, $G,m_0>0$, $m_i>0$, $0<a_1<\dots<a_n$, $\eta>0$, and $\zeta_*,d,D$ as in Definition~\ref{bpP:p6:tube-constants}. Then there is $\varepsilon_0>0$ such that for every $\varepsilon\in(0,\varepsilon_0)$ there is a Borel set $S_\varepsilon\subset\Ph{2n}$ with $0<\operatorname{meas}S_\varepsilon<\infty$, $S_\varepsilon\subset U_{\zeta_*}$, and every $z\in S_\varepsilon$ lies on some $\mathcal K:\R^{2n}\to\Ph{2n}$ with $\mathcal K(\R^{2n})\subset S_\varepsilon$ and $\operatorname{IsPhysTorus}(H_{\rm fix},w,\mathcal K)$, for $H_{\rm fix}=\texttt{fixedHamiltonian}\ G\ m_0\ m\ \varepsilon$. (Part~13 applies Proposition~\ref{bpP:p6:fixkol-assembly} to obtain $\operatorname{FixKol}(G,m_0,m,a,\varepsilon,\eta,d,D,S_\varepsilon)$ and the frozen P-FIXED.) The constants depend only on the data; the order of choice is: data $\to$ patch, $(\bar\mu,\bar\beta)$, $J_0,\gamma_0$ $\to$ $r_C,\sigma_F,r_z,M_F,L$ $\to$ $B_0,\kappa_D,a_D$ (Definition~\ref{bpP:p11:kam-input}(K5), for the fixed data $\mathfrak d_*$ of Remark~\ref{bpP:p11:data-star}) $\to$ $\nu_2$ $\to$ $\varepsilon_0$ $\to$ $\varepsilon$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{remark}[The data of the KAM input]\label{bpP:p11:data-star}
For $\varepsilon\le\varepsilon_3$ the Hamiltonian $\hat H_\varepsilon=N_\varepsilon+P_\varepsilon$ on $\T_{s_*}\times(K_\varepsilon+4\vartheta)$ is $\mathfrak d_\varepsilon$-admissible with $\mathfrak d_\varepsilon=(\bar\mu,\beta_\varepsilon,K_\varepsilon,\frac14,s_*,M_*,C_1,\tau_D,\frac12)$ (periodicity, reality and holomorphy: Proposition~\ref{bpP:p11:prepared}; (A1): Lemma~\ref{bpP:p11:amount}; (A2): Proposition~\ref{bpP:p11:prepared}(d); (A3): (b)). All entries but $\beta_\varepsilon=\tfrac12\beta_J\,\varepsilon^{\bar\mu+2}$ are independent of $\varepsilon$ ($K_\varepsilon=[-\frac12,\frac12]^{2n}$, $d_\varepsilon=\operatorname{diam}_\infty K_\varepsilon=1$, $\vartheta_\varepsilon=\frac14$); $\mathfrak d_*=(\bar\mu,\cdot,K_\varepsilon,\frac14,s_*,M_*,C_1,\tau_D,\frac12)$ denotes the common part to which (K5) is applied; $\tau_D>\max\{n\bar\mu-1,2n-1\}$.
\end{remark}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Theorem~\ref{bpP:p11:wrapper}]
\proofstep{Choice of $\nu_2$ and $\varepsilon_0$}\label{stepP:p11:wr-nu} Let $B_0,\kappa_D,a_D,c_*,c_D$ be the constants of (K5) for $\mathfrak d_*$. Choose $\nu_2:=\max\bigl\{(\bar\mu+2)B_0+2,\ \tfrac{(\bar\mu+2)a_D+1}{\kappa_D}+2\bigr\}$. By Proposition~\ref{bpP:p11:prepared}(a) $\varepsilon_f(\varepsilon)\le c_3\varepsilon^{\nu_2+3/2}\log^{c}\le\varepsilon^{\nu_2}$ for small $\varepsilon$, while $\varepsilon_{\rm adm}(\mathfrak d_\varepsilon)\ge c_*(\tfrac12\beta_J)^{B_0}\varepsilon^{(\bar\mu+2)B_0}\ge\varepsilon^{\nu_2-2}\cdot c'$; and $\Delta\le c_D\varepsilon_f^{\kappa_D}(\tfrac12\beta_J\varepsilon^{\bar\mu+2})^{-a_D}\le c''\varepsilon^{\kappa_D\nu_2-(\bar\mu+2)a_D}\le c''\varepsilon\le\frac12$. Hence there is $\varepsilon_0\le\varepsilon_3$ (depending only on the data, $\nu_2$ and the constants above) with: $\varepsilon_f(\varepsilon)\le\varepsilon_{\rm adm}(\mathfrak d_\varepsilon)$, $\Delta(\varepsilon_f;\mathfrak d_\varepsilon)\le\frac12$ and all smallness conditions of Propositions~\ref{bpP:p11:avg-fast}, \ref{bpP:p11:avg-slow}, \ref{bpP:p11:prepared}, \ref{bpP:p11:amount}, for every $\varepsilon\in(0,\varepsilon_0)$. This is the quantifier order demanded by R-CP-1 and A4: the admissible threshold is a \emph{lower} bound $c\varepsilon^{B}$, and $\nu_2$ is fixed \emph{before} $\varepsilon$.
\proofstep{The KAM input}\label{stepP:p11:wr-kam} Apply Definition~\ref{bpP:p11:kam-input} to $\hat H_\varepsilon$ (Remark~\ref{bpP:p11:data-star}) and $\varepsilon_f=\varepsilon_f(\varepsilon)$: compact $\mathcal H_\varepsilon\subset K_\varepsilon$, $\operatorname{meas}\mathcal H_\varepsilon\ge\frac12$, maps $\mathcal K_b=(\theta+u^\theta_b,\ \zeta+u^\zeta_b;\ r_b,\rho'_b)$ for $b=(b_r,b_{\rho'})\in\mathcal H_\varepsilon$ with defect $\abs{u_b}\le\frac12$, so the real points $(r,\rho')$ of the tori satisfy $\abs r_\infty,\abs{\rho'}_\infty\le1<\frac\pi2$ (the positions $\theta,\zeta$ are unrestricted by periodicity).
\proofstep{Pull-back through the chain of charts}\label{stepP:p11:wr-chain} Let $\Xi_\varepsilon:=\Psi_c\circ\Phi^1_\varepsilon\circ\hat\Theta\circ\Psi_2\circ A_\varepsilon$, defined on $\mathcal R_\varepsilon=\{(\theta,r,\zeta,\rho')\in\R^{4n}:\abs r_\infty<2,\ \abs{\rho'}_\infty<\frac\pi2\}$ (each factor is defined and real analytic there by the domain chains of Propositions~\ref{bpP:p11:avg-fast}, \ref{bpP:p11:avg-slow}, \ref{bpP:p11:holo-chart}, Lemma~\ref{bpP:p11:total-chart}), a $c$-chart with $c=\varepsilon$ (product of the conformal factors, all other factors $1$). Its lattice properties (Q1)--(Q2) with $\sigma_k=0$, $\iota_k=((k,0),0)$ shifting $(\theta,\zeta)$ by $k\in\Z^{2n}$, hold: $A_\varepsilon$ (angles unchanged), $\Psi_2$ (equivariant by Lemma~\ref{bpP:p11:aniso-avg}(a), injective), $\hat\Theta$ and $\Psi_c$ (Lemma~\ref{bpP:p11:total-chart}), $\Phi^1_\varepsilon$ ($\Phi^1-\mathrm{id}$ $\Z^n$-periodic in $\theta$, injective). By construction $H_{\rm fix}\circ\Xi_\varepsilon=H_2\circ A_\varepsilon=\varepsilon\hat H_\varepsilon$ on $\mathcal R_\varepsilon$ (Proposition~\ref{bpP:p11:holo-chart}(b), Propositions~\ref{bpP:p11:avg-fast}, \ref{bpP:p11:avg-slow}(c)), i.e.\ $\hat H_\varepsilon=H_{\rm fix}^{\Xi_\varepsilon}$ in the notation of Definition~\ref{bpP:p1:chart}.
\proofstep{Physical tori}\label{stepP:p11:wr-tor} For $b\in\mathcal H_\varepsilon$ the map $\theta\mapsto\mathcal K_b(\theta)\in\mathcal R_\varepsilon$ (Step~\ref{stepP:p11:wr-kam}) satisfies (L1)--(L6) of Definition~\ref{bpP:p1:lattice-torus} with $G=\hat H_\varepsilon$, $O=\mathcal R_\varepsilon$, $w=w_b$ and $\iota_k=((k,0),0)$ (here the $2n$ positions are $(\theta,\zeta)$ and $\mathcal K_b(\theta+k)=\mathcal K_b(\theta)+(k,0)$: (K2)); differentiability of $\hat H_\varepsilon$ is holomorphy. $w_b$ is $(\gamma,\tau_D)$-Diophantine with $\tau_D\ge2n>d-1$ for $d=2n$. Theorem~\ref{bpP:p1:torus-transport}(b) gives $\operatorname{IsPhysTorus}(H_{\rm fix},w_b,\Xi_\varepsilon\circ\mathcal K_b)$, and $(\Xi_\varepsilon\circ\mathcal K_b)(\R^{2n})=\Xi_\varepsilon(\mathcal K_b(\R^{2n}))$.
\proofstep{The set $S_\varepsilon$, measure}\label{stepP:p11:wr-meas} $A:=\mathcal K(\mathcal H_\varepsilon\times\R^{2n})$ is Borel, $\Z^{2n}$-invariant, and its unit cell $\mathcal K(\mathcal H_\varepsilon\times[0,1)^{2n})$ has $\operatorname{meas}\ge2^{-4n}\operatorname{meas}\mathcal H_\varepsilon>0$ (K4); put $S_\varepsilon=\Xi_\varepsilon(A)$. By Lemma~\ref{bpP:p6:measure-positive}(b) (a $c$-chart with (Q1)--(Q2), $c=\varepsilon$; $A\subset\mathcal R_\varepsilon$ open invariant) $S_\varepsilon$ is Borel and $\operatorname{meas}S_\varepsilon=\varepsilon^{2n}\operatorname{meas}(A\cap Q)>0$.
\proofstep{Inclusion in the tube}\label{stepP:p11:wr-tube} Let $(\theta,r,\zeta,\rho')\in A$: $\abs r_\infty\le1$, $\abs{\rho'}_\infty\le1$ (Step~\ref{stepP:p11:wr-kam}). Then The real action coordinates are $J=J_0+\varepsilon r$ and $\tilde\rho_j=\varepsilon(2\pi+\rho'_j)\in\varepsilon[2\pi-1,2\pi+1]$. Apply the displacements: $\Psi_2$ changes $\tilde\rho$ by $\le c_2\varepsilon^{3/2}\log^{\tau_0+1}$ and $J$ by $\le c_2\varepsilon^{5/2}\log^{2\tau_0+1}$ (Proposition~\ref{bpP:p11:avg-slow}(d)), so the image has $\tilde\rho_j\in\varepsilon[2\pi-2,2\pi+2]$, $\abs{J-J_0}_\infty\le2\varepsilon$ for small $\varepsilon$; $\Pi$ gives $\abs z_\infty^2\le\tilde\rho_j/\pi\le(2\pi+2)\varepsilon/\pi<3\varepsilon$; $\Phi^1_\varepsilon$ moves $J$ by $\le3\varepsilon M_F/(2\pi\alpha_1\sigma_F)\le c\varepsilon\log^{\tau_0}\frac1\varepsilon$ and $z$ by $\le9\varepsilon M_F/(2\pi\alpha_1r_z)\le c\varepsilon\log^{\tau_0}\frac1\varepsilon$ (Proposition~\ref{bpP:p11:avg-fast}); finally $(\xi,\eta)=\varrho(x,y)$ with $\sum_i(\xi_i^2+\eta_i^2)=\sum_j(x_j^2+y_j^2)<7n\varepsilon$ ($\varrho$ orthogonal, Proposition~\ref{bpP:p9:lift}), so $\Gamma_i=\frac12(\xi_i^2+\eta_i^2)\le4n\varepsilon$, and $\abs{\Lambda-\Lambda^*}_\infty\le\abs{\Lambda-\Lambda_0}+\abs{\Lambda_0-\Lambda^*}<\delta_*/8+c\varepsilon\log^{\tau_0}\frac1\varepsilon$ (patch choice). For $\varepsilon$ small the hypotheses of Lemma~\ref{bpP:p11:forward-tube} (with $\zeta=\zeta_*$, $\delta_{\zeta_*}=\delta_*$) hold, so $\Xi_\varepsilon(\theta,r,\zeta,\rho')=\Psi^{(n)}(\ldots)\in U_{\zeta_*}$, i.e.\ $S_\varepsilon\subset U_{\zeta_*}\subset U_\eta$ (Lemma~\ref{bpP:p6:tube-geometry}(a)). The decreasing choice of $\varepsilon_0$ so that these inequalities hold is added to Step~\ref{stepP:p11:wr-nu}.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Displacement in the polar and Birkhoff charts]\label{bpP:p11:displacement}
In the polar chart the oscillator actions $\tilde\rho_j$ of every torus lie in $\varepsilon[2\pi-2,2\pi+2]$ (Step~\ref{stepP:p11:wr-tube}), in particular they are positive, uniformly over the family; this is the quantitative form of CP09 Theorem~5 ($\bar w$ of norm $2\varepsilon$). There is no Birkhoff chart in this route (it is replaced by Step D); the rotation chart $\Phi_\varrho$ preserves $\Lambda$ and the Euclidean norms of $(\xi,\eta)$, which is what the tube needs (Step~\ref{stepP:p11:wr-tube}).
