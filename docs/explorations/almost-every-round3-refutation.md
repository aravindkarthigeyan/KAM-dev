## 1. P-EXP-SAFE

### 1a. Hamiltonian, angular momentum and the AMD identity: CONFIRMED

The displayed Hamiltonian was re-derived from canonical heliocentric (Poincaré) coordinates. The coordinates are r₀=u₀ and rᵢ=uᵢ−u₀, with conjugate momenta r̃₀=ΣUₖ and r̃ᵢ=Uᵢ. With Sun mass 1, G=1 and planet masses μmᵢ,

    H = Σ|r̃ᵢ|²/(2μmᵢ) + |Σr̃ᵢ|²/2 − Σμmᵢ/|rᵢ| − Σᵢ<ⱼ μ²mᵢmⱼ/|rᵢ−rⱼ|.

Substitute r̃ᵢ=μpᵢ. The form Σdr̃∧dr becomes μΣdp∧dq, so the same time variable is governed by H/μ, which is exactly the displayed Hμ, indirect term included. This is the "democratic heliocentric" split: the Kepler part keeps |pᵢ|²/(2mᵢ) and no reduced mass.

The match with the sources was checked:
- Figueras–Haro, [arXiv:2403.10152v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.10152v1) §2, eq. (2.1) (local `incoming-zohar2/r2-fhl24.pdf`), writes μ(Σ|yᵢ|²/2+Σᵢ<ⱼyᵢ·yⱼ−…), which equals μ(½|Σyᵢ|²−…).
- Laskar–Petit, eqs. (4)–(7), p. 2 (`r2-amd17.pdf`), use the same split, with H₁ containing ½‖ũ₀‖²/m₀.

Conservation of J follows from Noether's theorem. Every term of Hμ (|pᵢ|², |qᵢ|, |Σpᵢ|², |qᵢ−qⱼ|) is invariant under simultaneous rotation of all qᵢ and pᵢ, and that rotation is generated by Σqᵢ×pᵢ. The indirect term changes nothing. J equals (1/μ) times the total barycentric angular momentum, because the cotangent-lifted linear change of coordinates preserves Σu×U (LP17 eq. (9)).

The identity is exact in these variables. Each Kepler block is |p|²/(2m)−m/|q|. The osculating ellipse is built from the heliocentric position qᵢ and the velocity pᵢ/mᵢ, which is the planet's **barycentric** velocity, with central parameter 1. On that ellipse, qᵢ×pᵢ=mᵢ√(aᵢ(1−eᵢ²))=Λᵢ√(1−eᵢ²) and Λᵢ=mᵢ√aᵢ. Planar Poincaré variables give ½|zᵢ|²=Λᵢ−Gᵢ, so J=ΣΛᵢ−A.

Two conditions apply. Every orbit must be elliptic and prograde (Gᵢ>0), which is guaranteed inside the domain because A₁<ℓmin. These elements are not JPL heliocentric osculating elements, which use heliocentric velocity and G(1+μmᵢ). The two differ by O(μ) on compact collision-free sets. LP17 eq. (10)–(11) state the same identity for this split.

### 1b. AMD bootstrap: CONFIRMED

J(t)=J(0) gives the exact equation A(t)−A(0)=Σᵢ(Λᵢ(t)−Λᵢ(0)), so |A(t)−A(0)|≤n·|ΔΛ|∞. Only the upper bound matters, and it holds whatever the sign of each ΔΛᵢ. Drift that lowers A is harmless.

All boundary cases close under the stated choices Cμ*^β<½dist(Q₀,∂Q) and nCμ*^β<(A₁−A₀)/2:
- data with A(0)↑A₀;
- Λ(0) near ∂Q₀;
- z=0 (all circular);
- coherent drift with every ΔΛᵢ=+Cμ^β.

The per-planet step is also correct: Λᵢ(1−√(1−eᵢ²))≤A≤A₁<ℓmin(1−√(1−e*²))≤Λᵢ(1−√(1−e*²)). It needs Λᵢ(t)∈Q, which the same bootstrap supplies.

Energy plus J alone cannot replace Nekhoroshev, which I checked as a refutation attempt. With n=2, the energy level and J leave a one-parameter family along which ΣΛ, and hence A, can move freely. The argument therefore really does need fast-action confinement.

### 1c. The stopped properly degenerate theorem: CONFIRMED WITH CORRECTIONS, exponent UNVERIFIED

**(i) Pinzari D.1 is not stopped.** The brief points to `sources/pdf/arxiv_1501_pinzari_v4.pdf`, but that file is a different paper: Pinzari, *Perihelia reduction and global Kolmogorov tori*, arXiv:1501.04470v4. Its Appendix D contains Proposition D.1, a multi-scale normal form that specializes Pöschel's Normal Form Lemma, and Theorem D.4, a one-degree-of-freedom result. Neither is a Nekhoroshev theorem.

The theorem round 2 cites is in `incoming-zohar2/r2-pinzari13v3.pdf` (arXiv:1310.0181v3), Theorem D.1, p. 41. It reads: *"any trajectory … such that (p(t),q(t))∈B²ⁿ², ∀0≤t≤T:=(1/M)e^{(1/M)^a} verifies |I(t)−I(0)|≤r:=½M^b ∀0≤t≤T"*, with "one can find a, b∈(0,1)". Theorem 1.3, p. 7, eq. (11), has the same form. These are **whole-interval** hypotheses, with T fixed by M.

A whole-interval theorem cannot close the AMD bootstrap. Its hypothesis is the very confinement of z on all of [0,T] that the bootstrap is trying to prove. The Hamiltonian cannot be cut off analytically to make the hypothesis hold. The a priori speed bound |ż|=O(μ) only buys time of order 1/μ. Round 2's Lemma 4 says the positive-a,b Pinzari version "supports the same argument". As cited, that is false. The same goes for the §1 description of Pinzari as "controlling I until the slow variables leave the analytic domain".

**(ii) The stopped form is in the primary source.** Nekhoroshev, *Russian Math. Surveys* 32:6 (1977), [DOI 10.1070/RM1977v032n06ABEH003859](https://doi.org/10.1070/RM1977v032n06ABEH003859), local `incoming-round3/nek77-eng.pdf`, **Theorem 4.4, p. 30** (checked on the page image):

> Let I(t), p(t), φ(t), q(t) be an arbitrary (real) solution … such that I(0)∈G−d and p(t), q(t)∈D−d for all t∈[0,C], where C>0 is arbitrary. Then |I(t)−I(0)|<d/2 for all t∈[0, min[C,T]].

The theorem's data are as follows.
- **Exponents** (4.4)–(4.5): d=M^b, b=3/((12ζ+3s+14)α_{s−1}), T=(1/M)exp((1/M)^a), a=2/(12ζ+3s+14).
- **Smallness**: M=sup_F|grad H₁|. Remark 4.5.A, same page, allows sup|H₁| instead.
- **Domain** (§4.3.A, p. 29): F={Re I∈G, Re(p,q)∈D, |Im|<ρ}, where "G and D are arbitrary domains in Eˢ and E^{2(n−s)}". The slow domain therefore does **not** have to be a polydisc. Any domain with a uniform complex width ρ works, including the ball {A<A₁} or the per-planet polydisc {½|zᵢ|²<A₁ ∀i}. Both are collision-free by Lemma 2's per-planet argument, and both give a product domain Q×Tⁿ×D, as required.
- **Steepness**: §1.5, p. 5, says the parameters (p,q) "do not worsen the estimates". Concave h_K is quasi-convex, hence steep with all indices 1 (§1.18, p. 15; §12.5, p. 62). With indices 1, formula (4.1) on p. 28 gives ζ=s(s−1)/2; this is my evaluation, consistent with (12.4).

**(iii) The planetary bootstrap is Nekhoroshev's own.**
- Lemma 12.5.E (p. 62) states the stopped planetary form: for a solution on [0,τ] lying in W+δ₄, with "τ>0 arbitrary", |aᵢ(v(t))−aᵢ(v(0))|<C₂μ^b for t∈[0,min{τ,T}].
- Lemma 12.6.A (pp. 62–63) bounds |N(t)−N(0)|<C₃μ using total angular momentum.
- §1.18 (p. 15) says the planetary assertion "does not follow directly from the main theorem … We need to use … the integral of the moment of momentum".

Round 2's exact identity for J in heliocentric-canonical variables is cleaner than Nekhoroshev's O(μ) barycentric version, but it is the same mechanism.

**(iv) The exponent β=1/(2n) is UNVERIFIED.** Every source in hand was checked:
- **Guzzo 1999 IAU proceedings**, p. 443 (`r2-guzzo99-1.png`): ε^{1/(2n)} drift until min{T_e, t₀exp(ε₀/ε)^{1/(2n)}}. The text is real, but it is an informal sentence attributed to "Nekhoroshev 1977". That paper's exponents for a convex h are a=2/(12ζ+3s+14). Its planetary instance, eq. (12.4) on p. 56, is a=2/(6s²−3s+14): 1/16 for two planets, against 1/(2n)=1/4. The attribution is inaccurate, and the page is not a proof.
- **Guzzo 1999 RCD**, [DOI 10.1070/rd1999v004n02abeh000104](https://doi.org/10.1070/rd1999v004n02abeh000104) (`r2-guzzo99full.pdf`). The abstract (p. 78) and §1 (pp. 78–79) attribute the stopped bound with unspecified positive a, b. Lemma 1(ii) on p. 84 (page image checked) proves a stopped confinement up to min{T_Nek, T_e}, where T_e is the exit time of p from V. That proof sets N=(ε₀/ε)^{1/α} with α≥3n, and its time exponent is at most 1/(3n), not 1/(2n). Its slow variables (p,q)∈V×Tᵐ are action–angle (definition of D₂ρ, p. 82), which is singular at circular orbits z=0. Theorem 1 (p. 83) further assumes secular integrability and convexity.
- **Niederman 1996**, Nonlinearity 9, 1703–1751, [DOI 10.1088/0951-7715/9/6/017](https://doi.org/10.1088/0951-7715/9/6/017). The full text is paywalled and was not reached. The abstract was verified via the OpenAlex record, with Crossref confirming title, author and pages (`incoming-round3/n96-*.json`): *"Using a scheme given by Lochak, we derive constructively a Nekhorochev-like result of stability in the planetary n-body problem … bounds on the variation of the semi-major axes … we first extend the theorems of stability over exponentially long times in the case of nearly integrable degenerate systems."* The abstract gives no exponent.
- **Secondary attributions.** Féjoz–Guàrdia–Kaloshin–Roldán, [arXiv:1109.2892v2](https://arxiv.org/abs/1109.2892v2), §1.4.4, p. 9 (`incoming-round3/arxiv-1109.2892.pdf`), says Niederman "showed that the semi major axis obeys the above estimate … exp(cε^{−1/2n})". Clarke–Fejoz–Guardia (`sources/pdf/cfg24_laplace_lagrange_counterexample.pdf`, §1.3, p. 5) describe Niederman's result as conditional on the secular actions staying in a bounded region, and closed by angular momentum near circular coplanar orbits. Neither is a primary proof.

I believe β=1/(2n) is very likely true; this is a judgement, not a check. Pöschel's and Lochak's convex arguments transfer once the slow width is fixed: the slow-variable Cauchy losses are dominated by the small action radius, and energy conservation still gives |h(I(t))−h(I(0))|=O(μ) while z stays in the domain. Still, no primary text in hand states and proves it in Cartesian slow variables. It must not be frozen without either auditing Niederman 1996 or proving it inside the campaign.

**(v) Smallness.** M≤C₀μ by Cauchy estimates on a slightly larger domain. A smaller M only weakens the hypothesis "z∈D−d" and strengthens the conclusion, so using an upper bound for M is legitimate. Nekhoroshev's own planetary theorem takes M=C₁μ (eq. (12.4), p. 56).

### 1d. Analyticity: CONFIRMED (qualitative)

The planar Poincaré chart is real-analytic at zᵢ=0. The closed real set Q×Tⁿ×{½|zᵢ|²≤A₁} is compact and collision-free, with mutual distances above the radial gap and |qᵢ| at least aᵢ⁻(1−e*). So the squared distances Σ(qᵢ−qⱼ)ₖ² and Σqᵢₖ² stay off (−∞,0] on a uniform complex neighbourhood, and the square-root branches are analytic there.

Nekhoroshev Lemma 12.4.C (p. 58) is the spatial analogue, also proved by compactness. No numerical width has been computed; round 2 already says this. h_K has explicit complex Hessian bounds away from Λ=0, which also discharges hypothesis 4.3.B (p. 29).

### 1e. Continuation: CONFIRMED

On the strictly interior compact set the vector field is bounded and Lipschitz, so a maximal solution cannot end there. The first-exit contradiction uses continuity at the exit time. The barycentric Sun position −μΣmᵢqᵢ/(1+μΣmᵢ) is correct.

### Corrected statement (P-EXP-SAFE′)

The setting and region R are as in round 2 §2. There exist positive exponents a and b, and constants μ*, C, c, t₀, d, Rmax>0, depending only on the fixed data, with the following property. For every 0<μ<μ* and **every** initial point in R, the solution exists and is unique on [0, t₀exp(cμ^{−a})], and on that interval

- |Λ(t)−Λ(0)|∞≤Cμ^b;
- d≤|qᵢ(t)|≤Rmax and |qᵢ−qⱼ|≥d.

The primary source supports a=2/(6n²−3n+14) and b=3a/2. A frozen version should either assert ∃a,b>0 or fix exactly these values.

"β=1/(2n) for both" may be kept only as a separately named strengthening, pending audit. The polynomial-horizon corollary holds for any a>0.

The slow domain may be the ball {A<A₁} or the per-planet polydisc. The provider must be the **stopped** theorem: Nekhoroshev Thm 4.4 with C arbitrary, not Pinzari D.1.

**Each planet separately.** The summands are nonnegative, so each is at most A. Using its own Λᵢ, the bound is e_J≤0.0759 and e_S≤0.1210, both below e*=0.15.

**Conservation under the exact average.** Write the rotation as R_θ:(Λ,λ,z)↦(Λ,λ+θ𝟙,e^{∓iθ}z). Hμ∘R_θ=Hμ, so after the substitution λ→λ−θ𝟙 and translation invariance of Haar measure, ⟨Hμ⟩_λ(Λ,e^{∓iθ}z)=⟨Hμ⟩_λ(Λ,z). The averaged Hamiltonian is λ-free, so Λ is constant; it Poisson-commutes with J=ΣΛ−A, so A is constant.

The double average of the interaction is real-analytic wherever the two ellipses are radially separated. That holds on the whole invariant set {A≤A(0)}, since the gap is at least 1.995 au. Compact invariance therefore gives global existence. The indirect term averages to a function of Λ alone, because ⟨pᵢ⟩=0 over a Kepler period.

**Scope notes**, not defects:
- (i) This is Laskar's AMD-stability with a crude sufficient critical value. LP17 §2 and Appendix B give the sharper criterion, and round 2 itself cites them.
- (ii) It concerns the first-order (single-average) secular model. Near 5:2, second-order terms are known to matter quantitatively.
- (iii) The box is in democratic-heliocentric canonical elements; the JPL values differ by O(μ). Round 2 already marks the box as illustrative.

## 3. Round-1 averaging correction: CONFIRMED

AKN, 3rd ed., §6.1, Theorem 6.11 and Corollary 6.1, eqs. (6.27)–(6.28). The full-text transcription in `incoming-zohar2/akn06-web-excerpt.json` carries running head "6.1 Averaging of Perturbations 243". It states the rank condition (rank of I↦ω equals m, or rank of I↦[ω] equals m−1), then ∫max_{0≤t≤1/ε}|I(t)−J(t)|dI₀dφ₀<c₁√ε and meas E(ε,ρ)<c₁√ε/ρ, and continues:

> Equivalently: outside a set of measure κ the averaging error satisfies the estimate |I(t)−J(t)|<c₁√ε/κ. This estimate is best-possible [451].

So the brief's "error √ε outside a set of measure √ε" is not available from this theorem. Setting κ=√ε yields only an O(1) error bound, and AKN asserts the Markov form cannot be improved for the class. The details of the optimality example in Neishtadt 1976 are **unverified**. AKN contrasts this with c₂√ε|ln κ| for two-frequency systems under conditions A and B.

Independent check: `nei22slides.pdf`, slide 14/46, "Systems with many frequencies": the mean error is O(√ε), giving accuracy O(√ε/κ) off a set of measure κ, and κ=ε^{1/4} gives ε^{1/4} for both.

Minor correction to round 1: the slides are by **Neishtadt and Okunev**, not Neishtadt alone. The printed AKN page image remains unverified, as round 1 says.

Round 1's derivation that quasi-convexity implies projective rank m−1 is correct. If v⊥ω and P_{ω⊥}D²h·v=0, then D²h·v∥ω, so ⟨D²h·v,v⟩=0 and v=0. The L/ε extension by rescaling ε'=ε/L and f'=Lf is also correct.

## 4. Novelty: CONFIRMED known, with a sharper locator

**Nekhoroshev 1977, §12, Theorem 12.3, pp. 55–56** (page images checked) is the all-initial-data theorem. It covers the spatial (s+1)-body problem with masses μκᵢ and barycentric-frame elements.

- A "domain of planetary motions" B(α,β,γ) is defined by αᵢ≤aᵢ≤βᵢ, with 0<α₁≤β₁<α₂≤…≤βₛ, and |N|≥γ, where γ₀(α,β)<γ<γₘ(α,β).
- Here N is the sum of the planets' Kepler angular momenta divided by μ. γₘ corresponds to circular coplanar orbits, and γ₀ is the largest γ whose boundary meets a collision. The condition is exactly a low-AMD condition.
- **For every solution with v(0)∈B**: no collisions on [0,T], with T=(1/M)exp(M^{−a}), M=C₁μ and a=2/(6s²−3s+14). On that interval |aᵢ(t)−aᵢ(0)|<C₂μ^b with b=3a/2, and |N(t)−N(0)|<C₃μ.
- The exceptional set is empty.

Round 2's region R is not literally one such domain, because its deficit bound depends on Λ. It is covered by finitely many: around each point of R̄ take narrow a-intervals and γ slightly below N, which stays strictly above γ₀ by the per-planet argument. The planar problem is an invariant subsystem, and the barycentric and heliocentric-canonical elements differ by O(μ). So P-EXP-SAFE′ follows from Theorem 12.3.

Later results:
- **Niederman 1996** (abstract verified) re-proves and quantifies this result with Lochak's method.
- **Exponent attribution.** The 1/(2n) value is attributed to Niederman 1996 only secondarily (FGKR §1.4.4).
- **Pinzari's restatement.** Pinzari v3 (p. 8 and footnote 5, p. 7) restates the result as "for all initial data in phase space", with angular momentum as the closing ingredient.

Round 2's "known theorem, not a new dynamical result" is therefore correct. Its formalization value is a faithful machine-checked version of Nekhoroshev 1977, Theorem 12.3, in the planar case.

## 5. Source-fidelity notes for the ledger

1. **Pinzari, arXiv:1310.0181v3, Thm 1.3 (p. 7) and Thm D.1 (p. 41).** Both restate Nekhoroshev's Theorem 4.4 with C=T fixed and the slow-variable condition on all of [0,T]. This drops the source's "C>0 arbitrary … min[C,T]". The restatement is a valid special case, so it is not an error. It is, however, insufficient for the planetary bootstrap and must not serve as the provider pin.
2. **Guzzo 1999, IAU Colloq. 172 proceedings, p. 443.** Attributes drift ε^{1/(2n)} and time exp(ε₀/ε)^{1/(2n)} for convex h to "Nekhoroshev, 1977". That paper gives a=2/(12ζ+3s+14) (Theorem 4.4, p. 30), and a=2/(6s²−3s+14) in the planetary case (eq. (12.4), p. 56). This is a suspected attribution error.
3. **Brief pointer.** `sources/pdf/arxiv_1501_pinzari_v4.pdf` is arXiv:1501.04470v4. Its Appendix D.1 is a multi-scale normal form (Proposition D.1), not a Nekhoroshev Theorem D.1. The correct pin is `incoming-zohar2/r2-pinzari13v3.pdf`.
