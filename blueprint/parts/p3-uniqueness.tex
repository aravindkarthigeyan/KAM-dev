\section{Local uniqueness of invariant tori of flows (G2)}\label{bp:p3}

This part proves Theorem~\ref{bp:p0:g2}. Let $K_1$ and $K_2$ be invariant tori of $X_h$ with the same
Diophantine frequency~$\omega$, and let $K_1$ satisfy the nondegeneracy hypotheses of G1. If
$\abs{K_1-K_2}\le c\gamma^2\rho^{2\tau}$ on $T_\rho$, then $K_2=K_1(\cdot+\varphi)$ on $T_{\rho/2}$ for a
real phase~$\varphi$. No pinned source proves this for flows. GJLV05 prove it for exact symplectic maps
(Theorem~2, p.~862, proof in \S6, pp.~883--885) and do not formulate the flow version (Remark~7,
p.~865); HL19 assert it without proof (Remark~3.5, p.~18). We follow the map proof of GJLV05~\S6, with
the difference operator replaced by the derivative $\partial_\omega$ along~$\omega$ and the map frame
replaced by the flow frame of HL19~\S2.4--2.5 in the canonical case (GJLV05 \S8.2 uses the same frame
with $J=-\Omega_0$, its normal columns having the opposite sign). We do not pass through the
time-one map: the time-one rotation of a flow-Diophantine frequency can be resonant (for
$\omega=(1,\sqrt2)$ and $k=e_1$ one has $k\cdot\omega\in\Z$).

\paragraph{Strategy.} The proof has four steps.
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\arabic*)]
\item \emph{Exact geometry at $K_1$.} Since $K_1$ is exactly invariant, primary (so the pull-back of the
  symplectic form has zero average) and $\omega$ is nonresonant, $K_1$ is Lagrangian. Hence the frame $P=(L\ N)$ of HL19 Case~III is exactly symplectic and reduces the
  linearized vector field exactly to the triangular matrix
  $\Lambda=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}0&T\\0&0\end{smallmatrix}\right)$, where $T$ is the torsion
  (Subsection~\ref{bp:p3:geometry}).
\item \emph{Phase.} We translate $K_2$ by a real phase $\psi$ so that the tangential component of
  $K_2(\cdot+\psi)-K_1$ has zero average. This is a contraction in $\R^n$
  (Subsection~\ref{bp:p3:phase-section}).
\item \emph{Quadratic estimate.} The difference $\Delta=K_2(\cdot+\psi)-K_1$ solves the equation
  linearized at $K_1$ up to a remainder quadratic in~$\Delta$. In the frame~$P$ this equation becomes two
  cohomological equations. Their solutions are determined by the remainder once two averages are fixed:
  the tangential average vanishes by the choice of phase, and the normal average is fixed by the
  invertibility of~$\avg T$. The two small-divisor solutions cost $\gamma^{-2}$ together, and
  \begin{equation*}
  \norm{\Delta}_{\rho''}\le C_{\mathrm{quad}}\gamma^{-2}\bigl(\tfrac12(\rho'-\rho'')\bigr)^{-2(\tau+n)}
  \norm{\Delta}_{\rho'}^2\qquad\text{for }0<\rho''<\rho'\le\tfrac34\rho\,.
  \end{equation*}
  (Subsection~\ref{bp:p3:quadratic-section}.)
\item \emph{Iteration.} Applied on strips shrinking from $T_{3\rho/4}$ to $T_{\rho/2}$, the quadratic
  estimate drives $\norm{\Delta}$ to zero faster than the strip losses grow, so $\Delta=0$ on $T_{\rho/2}$
  (Subsection~\ref{bp:p3:proof-section}).
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{Why $c$ does not depend on $\gamma$.} The quadratic estimate loses exactly $\gamma^{-2}$,
which the closeness hypothesis $c\gamma^2\rho^{2\tau}$ compensates. The two other smallness conditions,
that the segment from $K_1(\theta)$ to $K_2(\theta+\psi)$ stays in $\mathfrak B$ and that the phase map
contracts, do not involve~$\gamma$. They follow from the closeness hypothesis because $\gamma$ is bounded
a priori: invariance of $K_1$ and the bounds on $DK_1$ and on the Gram inverse give $\abs\omega\le C_PM$,
and the Diophantine inequality for $k=e_1$ gives $\gamma\le\abs{\omega_1}$
(Lemma~\ref{bp:p3:frequency-bound}).

\subsection{Standing assumptions, notation and constants}\label{bp:p3:setting}

Throughout this part we fix $n\ge2$, $\tau\ge n-1$, $\rho>0$ and $M,\sigma_K,\sigma_B,\sigma_T,\mu>0$, as
in Theorem~\ref{bp:p0:g2}. Norms are those of Part~\ref{bp:p0}: $\abs{\cdot}$ is the max norm on $\C^m$
and the induced norm $\abs{A}=\max_i\sum_j\abs{A_{ij}}$ on matrices and linear maps, and
$\norm{u}_{\rho'}=\sup_{T_{\rho'}}\abs{u}$.

\begin{assumption}[Two invariant tori]\label{bp:p3:standing}
The data $\mathfrak B\subset\C^n\times\C^n$, $h:\C^n\times\C^n\to\C$,
$K_1,K_2:\C^n\to\C^n\times\C^n$, $\omega\in\R^n$ and $\gamma\in\R$ satisfy the following.
\begin{enumerate}[label=(S\arabic*)]
\item \emph{The Hamiltonian.} $\mathfrak B$ is open and invariant under $(\theta,I)\mapsto(\theta+k,I)$,
  $k\in\Z^n$; $h$ is holomorphic on $\mathfrak B$, $1$-periodic in~$\theta$ and real at the real points
  of~$\mathfrak B$; and $\abs{Dh(z)}$, $\abs{X_h(z)}$, $\abs{DX_h(z)}$, $\abs{D^2X_h(z)}\le M$ for every
  $z\in\mathfrak B$.
\item \emph{The frequency.} $\gamma>0$ and $\omega\in\Dio_{\gamma,\tau}$.
\item \emph{The reference torus.} $K_1$ is holomorphic on $T_\rho$, primary on $T_\rho$ and real on
  reals. For every $\theta\in T_\rho$: the ball of radius $\mu$ around $K_1(\theta)$ lies in
  $\mathfrak B$; $\abs{DK_1(\theta)}\le\sigma_K-\mu$; the Gram matrix $L^\top L(\theta)$ is invertible
  and $\abs{B(\theta)}\le\sigma_B-\mu$. The average $\avg T$ is invertible and
  $\abs{\avg T^{-1}}\le\sigma_T-\mu$. The invariance error $E_1$ of $K_1$ vanishes on $T_\rho$.
\item \emph{The second torus.} $K_2$ is holomorphic on $T_\rho$, primary on $T_\rho$, real on reals,
  $K_2(T_\rho)\subset\mathfrak B$, and its invariance error $E_2$ vanishes on $T_\rho$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{assumption}

These are the hypotheses of Theorem~\ref{bp:p0:g2} without the closeness hypothesis. Conditions (S1)
and (S2) contain Assumption~\ref{bp:p2:standing} of Part~\ref{bp:p2}, with the same
$n,\tau,M,\sigma_K,\sigma_B,\sigma_T$, so the results of Part~\ref{bp:p2} apply to $\mathfrak B$, $h$, $\omega$,
$\gamma$ and $K=K_1$; in the notation of Definition~\ref{bp:p2:objects}, $A=DX_h\circ K_1$ and
$\Omega_{K_1}=L^\top\Omega_0L$. The objects in (S3) are those of Definition~\ref{bp:p0:stripdata} for
$K=K_1$; we fix them now.
\begin{itemize}
\item The invariance errors are $E_i(\theta)=X_h(K_i(\theta))-DK_i(\theta)\omega$, $i=1,2$. For a
  function $u$ on a strip, $\partial_\omega u(\theta)\coloneqq Du(\theta)\omega=\sum_\ell\omega_\ell\,
  \partial_{\theta_\ell}u(\theta)$; for matrix-valued $u$ this is $Du(\theta)[\omega]$ of
  Definition~\ref{bp:p0:stripdata}.
\item $L\coloneqq DK_1$ ($2n\times n$), $B\coloneqq(L^\top L)^{-1}$, $N\coloneqq\Omega_0LB$,
  $P\coloneqq(L\ N)$ ($2n\times2n$), $X_N\coloneqq DX_h(K_1)N-\partial_\omega N$,
  $T\coloneqq N^\top\Omega_0X_N$, and $\avg T=\int_{[0,1]^n}T(\theta)\,d\theta$, the integral over real
  $\theta$. The transpose is bilinear (no complex conjugation).
\item For a $1$-periodic function $u$ continuous on $\R^n$, $\avg u\coloneqq\int_{[0,1]^n}u(\theta)\,
  d\theta$. This is the Fourier average $\hat u_0$ of Part~\ref{bp:p0}.
\item A vector $\xi\in\C^{2n}$ is written $\xi=(\xi^L,\xi^N)$ with $\xi^L,\xi^N\in\C^n$, so that
  $P\xi=L\xi^L+N\xi^N$: these are the tangential and normal coordinates of $P\xi$.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Constants.} All constants of this part are fixed here, before any data. They depend only
on $n$, $\tau$, $\rho$, $M$, $\sigma_K$, $\sigma_B$ and $\sigma_T$:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:p3:constants}
\begin{aligned}
C_P&\coloneqq2n\sigma_K(1+\sigma_B)\,,&\qquad\Gamma&\coloneqq C_PM\,,\\
C_{\mathrm{tor}}&\coloneqq C_P^2M\Bigl(1+\frac{4nC_P}{\rho}\Bigr)\,,&\qquad
C_{\mathrm{coh}}&\coloneqq c(n,\tau)\,,
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
where $c(n,\tau)$ is the constant of Lemma~\ref{bp:p1:cohomological}, and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:p3:Cquad}
\begin{aligned}
C^N&\coloneqq\tfrac12C_PM\bigl[(1+\sigma_TC_{\mathrm{tor}})C_{\mathrm{coh}}+\sigma_T\Gamma\rho^{\tau+n}
\bigr]\,,\qquad
C^L\coloneqq C_{\mathrm{coh}}\bigl[C_{\mathrm{tor}}C^N+\tfrac12C_PM\Gamma\rho^{\tau+n}\bigr]\,,\\
C_{\mathrm{quad}}&\coloneqq C_P\max\bigl\{C^L,\ \Gamma\rho^{\tau+n}C^N\bigr\}\,.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
Their roles: $C_P$ bounds the frame and its inverse (Lemma~\ref{bp:p3:matrix-norms}); $\Gamma$ bounds
$\abs\omega$ and $\gamma$ (Lemma~\ref{bp:p3:frequency-bound}); $C_{\mathrm{tor}}$ bounds the torsion on
$T_{3\rho/4}$ (Lemma~\ref{bp:p3:torsion-bound}); and $C_{\mathrm{quad}}$ is the constant of the
quadratic estimate (Proposition~\ref{bp:p3:quadratic}). The threshold $c$ of the theorem is chosen
in~\eqref{eq:p3:c} from these constants and~$\mu$.

\subsection{Auxiliary facts}\label{bp:p3:auxiliary}

The general facts on matrix norms, increments, Taylor bounds, averages and quadratic iteration used in
this part are those of Part~\ref{bp:p1}. The next lemma records the bounds on the frame of $K_1$.

\begin{lemma}[Matrix norms of the frame]\label{bp:p3:matrix-norms}
Assume (S3). For every $\theta\in T_\rho$:
\begin{gather*}
\abs{L}\le\sigma_K\,,\qquad\abs B\le\sigma_B\,,\qquad\abs{N}\le\sigma_K\sigma_B\,,\\
\abs{BL^\top}\le C_P\,,\qquad\abs{L^\top\Omega_0}\le C_P\,,\qquad\abs P\le C_P\,,\qquad
\Bigl|\begin{pmatrix}BL^\top\\-L^\top\Omega_0\end{pmatrix}\Bigr|\le C_P\,.
\end{gather*}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
\proofstep{The frame}\label{step:p3:norms-frame}
By (S3) and the convention that $\abs{DK_1(\theta)}$ is the operator norm, $\abs L\le\sigma_K$ and
$\abs B\le\sigma_B$. By Lemma~\ref{bp:p1:matrix-norms}, $\abs N=\abs{\Omega_0LB}=\abs{LB}\le\sigma_K\sigma_B$,
$\abs{BL^\top}\le\sigma_B\cdot2n\sigma_K\le C_P$ and $\abs{L^\top\Omega_0}=\abs{L^\top}\le2n\sigma_K\le C_P$, and
the stacked-block identity there bounds the last matrix by the maximum of the two previous bounds. By
Lemma~\ref{bp:p1:operator-norms} (one block row), $\abs P\le\abs L+\abs N\le\sigma_K(1+\sigma_B)\le C_P$.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Geometry of an invariant torus}\label{bp:p3:geometry}

This subsection specializes the geometry of Part~\ref{bp:p2} to the reference torus. The identities of
Lemmas~\ref{bp:p2:hamiltonian}(d), \ref{bp:p2:periodicity}, \ref{bp:p2:frame-identities}
and~\ref{bp:p2:exactness} hold for every torus, with its invariance error $E$; at $K_1$, $E_1=0$ makes
them exact.

\begin{lemma}[The reference torus is Lagrangian]\label{bp:p3:lagrangian}
Assume (S1)--(S3). Then $\Omega_{K_1}=L^\top\Omega_0L=0$ on $T_\rho$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
\proofstep{Invariance of $\Omega_{K_1}$ along the flow}\label{step:p3:lagrangian-transport}
By (S3), $K_1(T_\rho)\subset\mathfrak B$, $\det(L^\top L)\ne0$ and $E_1=0$ on the open set $T_\rho$, so
$DE_1=0$ there. Lemma~\ref{bp:p2:frame-identities}(d) gives $\partial_\omega\Omega_{K_1}=0$,
Lemma~\ref{bp:p2:periodicity} that $\Omega_{K_1}$ is $1$-periodic, and Lemma~\ref{bp:p2:exactness} that
$\avg{\Omega_{K_1}}=0$.

\proofstep{Nonresonance}\label{step:p3:lagrangian-unique}
The function $\Omega_{K_1}$ is holomorphic on $T_\rho$ and, by Lemmas~\ref{bp:p1:matrix-norms}
and~\ref{bp:p3:matrix-norms}, bounded there:
\begin{equation*}
\abs{\Omega_{K_1}}\le\abs{L^\top}\abs{\Omega_0L}\le2n\sigma_K^2\,.
\end{equation*}
Let $0<\delta<\rho$.
By the uniqueness part of Lemma~\ref{bp:p1:cohomological} with $g=0$ (which is applicable by (S2)),
the only function holomorphic and $1$-periodic on $T_{\rho-\delta}$ with zero average and
$Du\,\omega=0$ is $u=0$. Each entry of $\Omega_{K_1}$ is such a function, so $\Omega_{K_1}=0$ on
$T_{\rho-\delta}$. Since $\delta$ is arbitrary, $\Omega_{K_1}=0$ on $T_\rho$.
\end{proof}

\source{HL19 \S2.2, p.~7 (stated without proof for quasi-periodic tori), Lemma~4.3 with $E=0$;
GJLV05 Lemma~19(d), p.~890 ($L=0$ if $e=0$).}

\begin{lemma}[A priori bound on the frequency]\label{bp:p3:frequency-bound}
Assume (S1)--(S3). Then $\abs\omega\le\Gamma$ and $\gamma\le\Gamma$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
\proofstep{Bound on $\omega$}\label{step:p3:frequency-omega}
By (S3), $K_1(T_\rho)\subset\mathfrak B$, and $K_1$ satisfies \ref{bp:p2:A3} and \ref{bp:p2:A4} on $T_\rho$,
and \ref{bp:p2:A6} with $\varepsilon=0$. Lemma~\ref{bp:p2:frequency} with $\varepsilon=0$ gives
\begin{equation*}
\abs\omega\le2n\sigma_K\sigma_BM\le C_PM=\Gamma\,.
\end{equation*}

\proofstep{Bound on $\gamma$}\label{step:p3:frequency-gamma}
Lemma~\ref{bp:p2:frequency} also gives $\gamma\le\abs\omega$, by the Diophantine inequality of
Definition~\ref{bp:p0:diophantine} at $k=e_1$. Hence $\gamma\le\Gamma$.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Exact reducibility]\label{bp:p3:reducibility}
Assume (S1)--(S3). Then $B$, $N$ and $P$ are holomorphic on $T_\rho$, and for every $\theta\in T_\rho$
the frame $P(\theta)$ is invertible with
\begin{equation*}
P^{-1}=\begin{pmatrix}BL^\top\\-L^\top\Omega_0\end{pmatrix},\qquad P^\top\Omega_0P=\Omega_0\,.
\end{equation*}
Moreover $T=BL^\top X_N$ and, on $T_\rho$,
\begin{equation*}
DX_h(K_1)P-\partial_\omega P=P\Lambda\,,\qquad\Lambda\coloneqq\begin{pmatrix}0&T\\0&0\end{pmatrix}\,.
\end{equation*}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
\proofstep{Holomorphy}\label{step:p3:red-holomorphy}
$L=DK_1$ is holomorphic on $T_\rho$ (Lemma~\ref{bp:p1:holomorphic-regularity}), so $L^\top L$ is
holomorphic there and, by (S3), its determinant does not vanish. By
Lemma~\ref{bp:p1:holomorphic-inverse}, $B=(L^\top L)^{-1}$ is holomorphic on $T_\rho$, and so are
$N=\Omega_0LB$ and $P$.

\proofstep{Symplectic frame}\label{step:p3:red-frame}
By Lemma~\ref{bp:p3:lagrangian}, $\Omega_{K_1}=0$ on $T_\rho$, so $E_{\mathrm{sym}}=0$ and
$P^\top\Omega_0P=\Omega_0$ by Lemma~\ref{bp:p2:frame-identities}(b). By
Lemma~\ref{bp:p2:frame-identities}(f), $QP=I_{2n}$ with $Q=-\Omega_0P^\top\Omega_0$; as $P$ is square, it is
invertible with $P^{-1}=Q$. By Corollary~\ref{bp:p2:matrix}(d),
$Q=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}N^\top\Omega_0\\-L^\top\Omega_0\end{smallmatrix}\right)$, and
$N^\top\Omega_0=BL^\top\Omega_0^\top\Omega_0=BL^\top$, because $B$ is symmetric as the inverse of the
symmetric matrix $L^\top L$. This is the formula for $P^{-1}$, and it gives
$T=N^\top\Omega_0X_N=BL^\top X_N$.

\proofstep{Tangent columns}\label{step:p3:red-tangent}
Since $E_1=0$ on $T_\rho$, Lemma~\ref{bp:p2:frame-identities}(c) gives $\partial_\omega L=DX_h(K_1)L$, that
is, $X_L\coloneqq DX_h(K_1)L-\partial_\omega L=0$ on $T_\rho$.

\proofstep{Normal columns}\label{step:p3:red-normal}
Since $DE_1=0$, the matrix $E_{\mathrm{red}}$ of Lemma~\ref{bp:p2:frame-identities}(e) vanishes on
$T_\rho$, and that identity reads $Q\,(X_L\ X_N)=\Lambda$. Applying $Q^{-1}=P$ to the second block column,
$\left(\begin{smallmatrix}T\\0\end{smallmatrix}\right)$, gives $X_N=LT$.

\proofstep{Assembly}\label{step:p3:red-assembly}
The columns of $DX_h(K_1)P-\partial_\omega P$ are $(X_L\ X_N)=(0\ LT)$, and $P\Lambda=(L\ N)
\left(\begin{smallmatrix}0&T\\0&0\end{smallmatrix}\right)=(0\ LT)$.
\end{proof}

(Case~III: $N^0=\Omega_0L$), pp.~11--12; HL19, Lemma~4.10 with $E_{\mathrm{red}}=0$ when $E=0$,
pp.~29--30; GJLV05 Lemma~20, pp.~891--892 (the same frame with $J=-\Omega_0$, normal columns of
opposite sign).}

\begin{lemma}[The torsion]\label{bp:p3:torsion-bound}
Assume (S1)--(S3). Then $T$ is holomorphic on $T_\rho$, continuous on $\R^n$, and
$\norm{T}_{3\rho/4}\le C_{\mathrm{tor}}$. In particular $T$ is integrable on $[0,1]^n$, and $\avg T$ in
(S3) is the integral of a continuous function.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
\proofstep{Holomorphy}\label{step:p3:torsion-holomorphy}
By Lemma~\ref{bp:p3:reducibility}, $N$ is holomorphic on $T_\rho$, hence so is $\partial_\omega N$; and
$\theta\mapsto DX_h(K_1(\theta))$ is holomorphic on $T_\rho$ because $K_1(T_\rho)\subset\mathfrak B$ and
$DX_h$ is holomorphic on $\mathfrak B$ (Lemma~\ref{bp:p2:hamiltonian}(a)). Hence $X_N$ and $T=BL^\top X_N$ are holomorphic on $T_\rho$, so
continuous on $\R^n\subset T_\rho$.

\proofstep{Bound}\label{step:p3:torsion-bound-step}
By Lemma~\ref{bp:p3:matrix-norms}, $\norm N_\rho\le\sigma_K\sigma_B$. Lemma~\ref{bp:p1:cauchy-strip}
with $\delta=\rho/4$ gives $\norm{\partial_{\theta_\ell}N}_{3\rho/4}\le4\sigma_K\sigma_B/\rho$, so by
Lemma~\ref{bp:p3:frequency-bound}
\begin{equation*}
\norm{\partial_\omega N}_{3\rho/4}\le\sum_\ell\abs{\omega_\ell}\norm{\partial_{\theta_\ell}N}_{3\rho/4}
\le\frac{4n\Gamma\sigma_K\sigma_B}{\rho}\,.
\end{equation*}
With $\abs{DX_h}\le M$, $\Gamma=C_PM$, $\abs{BL^\top}\le C_P$ and $\sigma_K\sigma_B\le C_P$,
\begin{equation*}
\norm{X_N}_{3\rho/4}\le\sigma_K\sigma_BM\Bigl(1+\frac{4nC_P}{\rho}\Bigr)\,,\qquad
\norm T_{3\rho/4}\le\abs{BL^\top}\norm{X_N}_{3\rho/4}\le C_P^2M\Bigl(1+\frac{4nC_P}{\rho}\Bigr)
=C_{\mathrm{tor}}\,.
\end{equation*}
\end{proof}

\subsection{Phase normalization}\label{bp:p3:phase-section}

The invariance equation does not determine a torus: with $K_2$, every translate $K_2(\cdot+\psi)$,
$\psi\in\R^n$, is invariant with frequency $\omega$. In the frame of $K_1$, a small translation changes
the tangential coordinate $BL^\top(K_2(\cdot+\psi)-K_1)$ by approximately $\psi$, since
$BL^\top L=I$. Hence a unique small $\psi$ makes its average vanish. This is the normalization that
the quadratic estimate needs. Since all functions involved are real on reals, the fixed-point problem
lives in $\R^n$, which makes the phase real.

\begin{lemma}[Phase normalization]\label{bp:p3:phase}
Assume (S1)--(S4), and let $\eta\coloneqq\sup_{\theta\in T_\rho}\abs{K_2(\theta)-K_1(\theta)}$ satisfy
\begin{equation}\label{eq:p3:phase-small}
\eta\le\frac{\rho}{4nC_P(1+2C_P\sigma_K)}\,.
\end{equation}
Then there exists $\psi\in\R^n$ with $\abs\psi\le2C_P\eta$ such that
\begin{equation*}
\Bigl\langle BL^\top\bigl(K_2(\cdot+\psi)-K_1\bigr)\Bigr\rangle=0\,.
\end{equation*}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Write $W\coloneqq K_2-K_1$, which is holomorphic and $1$-periodic on $T_\rho$ (both tori are primary)
and bounded there by $\eta$.

\proofstep{Realness}\label{step:p3:phase-real}
For real $\theta$, $K_1(\theta)$ and $K_2(\theta)$ are real by (S3)--(S4). The partial derivatives of
$K_1$ at real $\theta$ are limits of real difference quotients along real directions, so $L(\theta)$ is
real; hence $L^\top L(\theta)$ and its inverse $B(\theta)$ are real (Lemma~\ref{bp:p1:real-inverse}). Consequently, for every
$\psi\in\R^n$ the functions $\theta\mapsto B(\theta)L(\theta)^\top W(\theta+\psi)$ and
$\theta\mapsto B(\theta)L(\theta)^\top\bigl(K_1(\theta+\psi)-K_1(\theta)-L(\theta)\psi\bigr)$ are real
and continuous on $\R^n$, and their averages are real.

\proofstep{Fixed-point form}\label{step:p3:phase-fixed-point}
For $\theta,\psi\in\R^n$, using $BL^\top L=I$ (the definition of $B$),
\begin{equation*}
BL^\top(\theta)\bigl(K_2(\theta+\psi)-K_1(\theta)\bigr)=\psi+BL^\top(\theta)\Bigl(W(\theta+\psi)
+K_1(\theta+\psi)-K_1(\theta)-L(\theta)\psi\Bigr)\,.
\end{equation*}
Define $\Phi:\R^n\to\R^n$ by
\begin{equation*}
\Phi(\psi)\coloneqq-\Bigl\langle BL^\top\bigl(W(\cdot+\psi)+K_1(\cdot+\psi)-K_1-L\psi\bigr)\Bigr\rangle\,.
\end{equation*}
The normalization of the lemma holds if and only if $\Phi(\psi)=\psi$.

\proofstep{Derivative bounds on the real torus}\label{step:p3:phase-derivatives}
By Lemma~\ref{bp:p1:increments} on $T_\rho$ with $\delta=\rho/2$, applied to $W$ (bounded by $\eta$)
and to $L$ (holomorphic by Lemma~\ref{bp:p1:holomorphic-regularity}, bounded by $\sigma_K$), for $\theta\in\R^n$ and $y,y'\in\R^n$,
\begin{equation*}
\abs{W(\theta+y)-W(\theta+y')}\le\frac{2n\eta}{\rho}\abs{y-y'}\,,\qquad
\abs{L(\theta+y)-L(\theta)}\le\frac{2n\sigma_K}{\rho}\abs{y}\,.
\end{equation*}

\proofstep{Contraction}\label{step:p3:phase-contraction}
Let $Y\coloneqq\{\psi\in\R^n:\abs\psi\le2C_P\eta\}$ and $\psi,\psi'\in Y$. For real $\theta$,
\begin{equation*}
K_1(\theta+\psi)-K_1(\theta+\psi')-L(\theta)(\psi-\psi')=\int_0^1\bigl(L(\theta+\psi'+t(\psi-\psi'))
-L(\theta)\bigr)(\psi-\psi')\,dt\,,
\end{equation*}
and $\abs{\psi'+t(\psi-\psi')}\le2C_P\eta$, so by Step~\ref{step:p3:phase-derivatives} its norm is at
most $(2n\sigma_K/\rho)\,2C_P\eta\abs{\psi-\psi'}$. Together with the bound on $W$ and
$\abs{BL^\top}\le C_P$,
\begin{equation*}
\abs{\Phi(\psi)-\Phi(\psi')}\le C_P\Bigl(\frac{2n\eta}{\rho}+\frac{4nC_P\sigma_K\eta}{\rho}\Bigr)
\abs{\psi-\psi'}=\frac{2nC_P\eta(1+2C_P\sigma_K)}{\rho}\abs{\psi-\psi'}\le\frac12\abs{\psi-\psi'}
\end{equation*}
by~\eqref{eq:p3:phase-small}. Moreover $\Phi(0)=-\avg{BL^\top W}$, so $\abs{\Phi(0)}\le C_P\eta$, and
for $\psi\in Y$, $\abs{\Phi(\psi)}\le C_P\eta+\frac12\abs\psi\le2C_P\eta$. Thus $\Phi$ maps the closed
set $Y$ into itself and is a contraction there.

\proofstep{Conclusion}\label{step:p3:phase-conclusion}
By the Banach fixed point theorem~\ref{bp:p1:ext-banach} in the complete metric space $Y$, there exists $\psi\in Y$ with
$\Phi(\psi)=\psi$, which is the normalization by Step~\ref{step:p3:phase-fixed-point}.
\end{proof}

\subsection{The quadratic estimate}\label{bp:p3:quadratic-section}

Fix a phase $\psi\in\R^n$ and put
\begin{equation}\label{eq:p3:Delta}
\Delta(\theta)\coloneqq K_2(\theta+\psi)-K_1(\theta)\,,\qquad\theta\in T_\rho\,.
\end{equation}
Both $K_1$ and $K_2(\cdot+\psi)$ solve the invariance equation, so their difference solves the equation
linearized at $K_1$ up to a quadratic remainder. In the frame $P$ the linearized operator is the
triangular operator $\partial_\omega-\Lambda$, by Lemma~\ref{bp:p3:reducibility}. This is the flow
analogue of GJLV05 (77) and Proposition~8 (pp.~875--876, 884).

\begin{lemma}[The difference in the frame]\label{bp:p3:difference-equation}
Assume (S1)--(S4), let $\psi\in\R^n$ and let $\Delta$ be given by~\eqref{eq:p3:Delta}, with
$\norm{\Delta}_\rho<\mu$. Define on $T_\rho$
\begin{equation*}
R\coloneqq X_h(K_1+\Delta)-X_h(K_1)-DX_h(K_1)\Delta\,,\qquad\xi\coloneqq P^{-1}\Delta\,,\qquad
q\coloneqq P^{-1}R\,,
\end{equation*}
so that $\xi^L=BL^\top\Delta$, $\xi^N=-L^\top\Omega_0\Delta$, $q^L=BL^\top R$ and
$q^N=-L^\top\Omega_0R$. Then the following hold on $T_\rho$.
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\alph*)]
\item $\Delta$, $\xi$ and $q$ are holomorphic; $\Delta$ and $\xi$ are $1$-periodic; $\Delta=P\xi$.
\item $\partial_\omega\xi^L=T\xi^N+q^L$ and $\partial_\omega\xi^N=q^N$.
\item $\abs{\xi}\le C_P\abs\Delta$, $\abs\Delta\le C_P\abs\xi$ and $\abs q\le\frac12C_PM\abs\Delta^2$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
\proofstep{Regularity and periodicity}\label{step:p3:diff-regularity}
Since $\psi$ is real, $T_\rho+\psi=T_\rho$, so $K_2(\cdot+\psi)$ is holomorphic on $T_\rho$, and so is
$\Delta$. For $\theta\in T_\rho$ the point $K_1(\theta)+\Delta(\theta)=K_2(\theta+\psi)$ lies in the ball
of radius $\mu$ around $K_1(\theta)$, and so does the whole segment from $K_1(\theta)$ to it; this ball
lies in $\mathfrak B$ by (S3). Hence $X_h(K_1+\Delta)$, and with it $R$, is holomorphic on $T_\rho$. By
primarity of $K_1$ and $K_2$, $\Delta(\theta+k)=K_2(\theta+\psi)+(k,0)-K_1(\theta)-(k,0)=\Delta(\theta)$.
By Lemma~\ref{bp:p3:reducibility}, $P^{-1}=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}BL^\top\\-L^\top\Omega_0
\end{smallmatrix}\right)$ is holomorphic on $T_\rho$, and it is $1$-periodic because $L$ is
(Lemma~\ref{bp:p2:periodicity}). So $\xi$ and $q$ are holomorphic and $\xi$ is $1$-periodic. Finally $P\xi=PP^{-1}\Delta=\Delta$.

\proofstep{Linearized equation}\label{step:p3:diff-linearized}
By (S3)--(S4), $\partial_\omega K_1=X_h(K_1)$ and $\partial_\omega\bigl(K_2(\cdot+\psi)\bigr)(\theta)
=DK_2(\theta+\psi)\omega=X_h(K_2(\theta+\psi))$ on $T_\rho$. Subtracting,
\begin{equation*}
\partial_\omega\Delta=X_h(K_1+\Delta)-X_h(K_1)=DX_h(K_1)\Delta+R\,.
\end{equation*}

\proofstep{Triangular system}\label{step:p3:diff-triangular}
Insert $\Delta=P\xi$: $\partial_\omega\Delta=(\partial_\omega P)\xi+P\partial_\omega\xi$, while
$DX_h(K_1)P\xi=(\partial_\omega P+P\Lambda)\xi$ by Lemma~\ref{bp:p3:reducibility}. Hence
$P\partial_\omega\xi=P\Lambda\xi+R$, and multiplying by $P^{-1}$, $\partial_\omega\xi=\Lambda\xi+q$.
Since $\Lambda\xi=(T\xi^N,0)$, this is~(b).

\proofstep{Bounds}\label{step:p3:diff-bounds}
The bounds on $\xi$ and $\Delta$ follow from $\abs{P^{-1}}\le C_P$ and $\abs P\le C_P$
(Lemma~\ref{bp:p3:matrix-norms}). Lemma~\ref{bp:p1:taylor}, applied to $F=X_h$, which is holomorphic on
$U=\mathfrak B$ (Lemma~\ref{bp:p2:hamiltonian}(a)), with $A=M$, $z=K_1(\theta)$ and $w=\Delta(\theta)$, whose
segment lies in $\mathfrak B$ by Step~\ref{step:p3:diff-regularity}, gives $\abs R\le\frac12M\abs\Delta^2$.
Here $\abs{D^2X_h}$ is the norm of the bilinear map (Lean \Lean{iteratedFDeriv}), which equals
$\abs{D(DX_h)}$ by Lemma~\ref{bp:p1:holomorphic-regularity}. Hence $\abs q\le\frac12C_PM\abs\Delta^2$.
\end{proof}

The next proposition is the approximate left inverse of the linearized operator (GJLV05 \S6, after
Zehnder). The normal coordinate $\xi^N$ is determined by its equation up to its average; that average
is fixed by the requirement that the right side of the tangential equation have zero average, which
uses the invertibility of $\avg T$. The tangential coordinate $\xi^L$ is then determined up to its
average, which the phase normalization sets to zero. Each of the two cohomological equations costs
$\gamma^{-1}\delta^{-(\tau+n)}$, and the remainder is quadratic.

\begin{proposition}[Quadratic estimate]\label{bp:p3:quadratic}
Assume (S1)--(S4). Let $\psi\in\R^n$ and let $\Delta$ be given by~\eqref{eq:p3:Delta}, with
\begin{equation*}
\norm{\Delta}_\rho<\mu\qquad\text{and}\qquad\avg{BL^\top\Delta}=0\,.
\end{equation*}
Then for every $0<\rho''<\rho'\le\frac34\rho$, with $\delta\coloneqq\frac12(\rho'-\rho'')$ and
$C_{\mathrm{quad}}$ given by~\eqref{eq:p3:Cquad},
\begin{equation*}
\norm{\Delta}_{\rho''}\le C_{\mathrm{quad}}\gamma^{-2}\delta^{-2(\tau+n)}\norm{\Delta}_{\rho'}^2\,.
\end{equation*}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Let $\xi$ and $q$ be as in Lemma~\ref{bp:p3:difference-equation}. Since $\norm{\Delta}_\rho<\mu$, the
functions $\Delta$, $\xi$ and $q$ are bounded on $T_\rho$ by Lemma~\ref{bp:p3:difference-equation}(c). We use repeatedly that $\delta<\rho$
and $\gamma\le\Gamma$ (Lemma~\ref{bp:p3:frequency-bound}), so that
\begin{equation}\label{eq:p3:absorb}
1\le\Gamma\rho^{\tau+n}\,\gamma^{-1}\delta^{-(\tau+n)}\,.
\end{equation}

\proofstep{The remainder}\label{step:p3:quad-remainder}
By Lemma~\ref{bp:p3:difference-equation}(c), $\norm{q}_{\rho'}\le\frac12C_PM\norm{\Delta}_{\rho'}^2$.

\proofstep{Oscillating part of the normal coordinate}\label{step:p3:quad-normal-osc}
The function $\xi^N$ is holomorphic, bounded and $1$-periodic on $T_{\rho'}$, and
$\partial_\omega\xi^N=q^N$. Hence $q^N$ is holomorphic, bounded and $1$-periodic on $T_{\rho'}$, and
$\avg{q^N}=\avg{\partial_\omega\xi^N}=0$ by Lemma~\ref{bp:p1:averages}. The function $\tilde\xi^N\coloneqq\xi^N-\avg{\xi^N}$ is holomorphic and
$1$-periodic on $T_{\rho'-\delta}$, has zero average and solves $D\tilde\xi^N\omega=q^N$. By the
uniqueness in Lemma~\ref{bp:p1:cohomological} (on $T_{\rho'}$, loss $\delta$), $\tilde\xi^N$ is the
solution given there, so
\begin{equation*}
\norm{\tilde\xi^N}_{\rho'-\delta}\le C_{\mathrm{coh}}\gamma^{-1}\delta^{-(\tau+n)}\norm{q^N}_{\rho'}
\le\tfrac12C_PMC_{\mathrm{coh}}\gamma^{-1}\delta^{-(\tau+n)}\norm{\Delta}_{\rho'}^2\,.
\end{equation*}

\proofstep{Average of the normal coordinate}\label{step:p3:quad-normal-avg}
The function $\xi^L$ is holomorphic, bounded and $1$-periodic on $T_\rho$, so
$\avg{\partial_\omega\xi^L}=0$ by Lemma~\ref{bp:p1:averages}. Averaging
$\partial_\omega\xi^L=T\xi^N+q^L$ over $[0,1]^n$ and splitting $\xi^N=\avg{\xi^N}+\tilde\xi^N$ gives
$0=\avg T\avg{\xi^N}+\avg{T\tilde\xi^N}+\avg{q^L}$. The matrix $\avg T$ is invertible by (S3), so
\begin{equation*}
\avg{\xi^N}=-\avg T^{-1}\bigl(\avg{T\tilde\xi^N}+\avg{q^L}\bigr)\,.
\end{equation*}
The averages involve only real $\theta$, where $\abs T\le C_{\mathrm{tor}}$
(Lemma~\ref{bp:p3:torsion-bound}). Hence
$\abs{\avg{\xi^N}}\le\sigma_T\bigl(C_{\mathrm{tor}}\norm{\tilde\xi^N}_{\rho'-\delta}
+\frac12C_PM\norm{\Delta}_{\rho'}^2\bigr)$.

\proofstep{The normal coordinate}\label{step:p3:quad-normal}
Adding the last two steps and using~\eqref{eq:p3:absorb} on the term without small divisor,
\begin{equation*}
\norm{\xi^N}_{\rho'-\delta}\le(1+\sigma_TC_{\mathrm{tor}})\norm{\tilde\xi^N}_{\rho'-\delta}
+\tfrac12\sigma_TC_PM\norm{\Delta}_{\rho'}^2\le C^N\gamma^{-1}\delta^{-(\tau+n)}\norm{\Delta}_{\rho'}^2\,.
\end{equation*}

\proofstep{The tangential coordinate}\label{step:p3:quad-tangent}
The function $g\coloneqq\partial_\omega\xi^L=T\xi^N+q^L$ is holomorphic and $1$-periodic on
$T_{\rho'-\delta}$, has zero average by Step~\ref{step:p3:quad-normal-avg}, and is bounded there because
$T_{\rho'-\delta}\subset T_{3\rho/4}$:
\begin{equation*}
\norm{g}_{\rho'-\delta}\le C_{\mathrm{tor}}\norm{\xi^N}_{\rho'-\delta}+\norm{q^L}_{\rho'}
\le\bigl(C_{\mathrm{tor}}C^N+\tfrac12C_PM\Gamma\rho^{\tau+n}\bigr)\gamma^{-1}\delta^{-(\tau+n)}
\norm{\Delta}_{\rho'}^2\,,
\end{equation*}
by Step~\ref{step:p3:quad-normal} and~\eqref{eq:p3:absorb}. Since $\avg{\xi^L}=\avg{BL^\top\Delta}=0$
by hypothesis, $\xi^L$ is holomorphic and $1$-periodic on $T_{\rho''}=T_{\rho'-2\delta}$, has zero
average and solves $D\xi^L\omega=g$. By the uniqueness in Lemma~\ref{bp:p1:cohomological} (on
$T_{\rho'-\delta}$, loss $\delta$),
\begin{equation*}
\norm{\xi^L}_{\rho''}\le C_{\mathrm{coh}}\gamma^{-1}\delta^{-(\tau+n)}\norm{g}_{\rho'-\delta}
\le C^L\gamma^{-2}\delta^{-2(\tau+n)}\norm{\Delta}_{\rho'}^2\,.
\end{equation*}

\proofstep{Return to $\Delta$}\label{step:p3:quad-combine}
By Step~\ref{step:p3:quad-normal} and~\eqref{eq:p3:absorb},
\begin{equation*}
\norm{\xi^N}_{\rho''}\le\norm{\xi^N}_{\rho'-\delta}\le\Gamma\rho^{\tau+n}C^N\gamma^{-2}
\delta^{-2(\tau+n)}\norm{\Delta}_{\rho'}^2\,.
\end{equation*}
Since $\Delta=P\xi$ and $\abs P\le C_P$,
\begin{equation*}
\norm{\Delta}_{\rho''}\le C_P\max\bigl\{\norm{\xi^L}_{\rho''},\norm{\xi^N}_{\rho''}\bigr\}
\le C_P\max\bigl\{C^L,\Gamma\rho^{\tau+n}C^N\bigr\}\gamma^{-2}\delta^{-2(\tau+n)}\norm{\Delta}_{\rho'}^2\,,
\end{equation*}
which is the claim, by the definition~\eqref{eq:p3:Cquad} of $C_{\mathrm{quad}}$.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Proof of Theorem~\ref{bp:p0:g2}}\label{bp:p3:proof-section}

The quadratic estimate loses a strip of width $2\delta$ at each application. Losing a geometrically
decreasing width $2\delta_m=2^{-m-3}\rho$ at the $m$-th application costs a factor
$\delta_m^{-2(\tau+n)}$ that grows only geometrically in $m$, while squaring makes the norms decrease
super-exponentially. Lemma~\ref{bp:p1:iteration} records the comparison.

\begin{proof}[Proof of Theorem~\ref{bp:p0:g2}]
\proofstep{Choice of the threshold}\label{step:p3:g2-constant}
With the constants~\eqref{eq:p3:constants}--\eqref{eq:p3:Cquad}, put
\begin{equation}\label{eq:p3:c}
c\coloneqq\frac{1}{1+2C_P\sigma_K}\min\Bigl\{\frac{\rho^{1-2\tau}}{4nC_P\Gamma^2},\
\frac{\mu\rho^{-2\tau}}{2\Gamma^2},\ \frac{(\rho/32)^{2(\tau+n)}\rho^{-2\tau}}{2C_{\mathrm{quad}}}\Bigr\}\,.
\end{equation}
Then $c>0$, and $c$ depends only on $n$, $\tau$, $\rho$, $M$, $\sigma_K$, $\sigma_B$, $\sigma_T$ and $\mu$; in particular not on
$\gamma$. Let $\mathfrak B$, $h$, $K_1$, $K_2$, $\omega$, $\gamma$ satisfy the hypotheses of the theorem. They satisfy
Assumption~\ref{bp:p3:standing}, and the closeness hypothesis reads
\begin{equation*}
\eta\coloneqq\sup_{\theta\in T_\rho}\abs{K_2(\theta)-K_1(\theta)}\le c\gamma^2\rho^{2\tau}\,.
\end{equation*}

\proofstep{The three smallness conditions}\label{step:p3:g2-smallness}
By Lemma~\ref{bp:p3:frequency-bound}, $\gamma\le\Gamma$, so $\eta\le c\Gamma^2\rho^{2\tau}$. The first
two terms of the minimum in~\eqref{eq:p3:c} give
\begin{equation*}
\eta\le\frac{\rho}{4nC_P(1+2C_P\sigma_K)}\qquad\text{and}\qquad(1+2C_P\sigma_K)\,\eta\le\frac\mu2\,,
\end{equation*}
and the third, used with $\gamma$ itself, gives
$(1+2C_P\sigma_K)\,\eta\le\gamma^2(\rho/32)^{2(\tau+n)}/(2C_{\mathrm{quad}})$.

\proofstep{Phase}\label{step:p3:g2-phase}
By the first condition and Lemma~\ref{bp:p3:phase}, there exists $\psi\in\R^n$ with
$\abs\psi\le2C_P\eta$ and $\avg{BL^\top\Delta}=0$, where $\Delta=K_2(\cdot+\psi)-K_1$ as
in~\eqref{eq:p3:Delta}.

\proofstep{Size of the difference}\label{step:p3:g2-size}
For $\theta\in T_\rho$, also $\theta+\psi\in T_\rho$, and
$\Delta(\theta)=\bigl(K_2-K_1\bigr)(\theta+\psi)+\bigl(K_1(\theta+\psi)-K_1(\theta)\bigr)$. The first
term is at most $\eta$; the second is at most $\sigma_K\abs\psi$ by Lemma~\ref{bp:p1:increments-open} on the convex open set
$T_\rho$, with $\abs{DK_1}\le\sigma_K$ there. Hence, by the second condition,
\begin{equation*}
\norm{\Delta}_\rho\le\eta+2C_P\sigma_K\eta=(1+2C_P\sigma_K)\,\eta\le\frac\mu2<\mu\,.
\end{equation*}

\proofstep{Iteration on shrinking strips}\label{step:p3:g2-iteration}
Let $\rho_m\coloneqq\bigl(\frac12+2^{-m-2}\bigr)\rho$, so $\rho_0=\frac34\rho$, $\rho_m$ decreases to
$\frac12\rho$, and $\frac12(\rho_m-\rho_{m+1})=2^{-m-4}\rho$. Put $y_m\coloneqq\norm{\Delta}_{\rho_m}$.
Proposition~\ref{bp:p3:quadratic} with $\rho'=\rho_m$ and $\rho''=\rho_{m+1}$ applies by the previous two
steps and gives
\begin{equation*}
y_{m+1}\le C_{\mathrm{quad}}\gamma^{-2}\bigl(2^{-m-4}\rho\bigr)^{-2(\tau+n)}y_m^2=Q2^{pm}y_m^2\,,
\end{equation*}
with $Q\coloneqq C_{\mathrm{quad}}\gamma^{-2}(\rho/16)^{-2(\tau+n)}$ and $p\coloneqq2(\tau+n)$.
By Step~\ref{step:p3:g2-size} and the third condition,
\begin{equation*}
Q2^py_0\le C_{\mathrm{quad}}\gamma^{-2}(\rho/32)^{-2(\tau+n)}(1+2C_P\sigma_K)\,\eta\le\frac12\,.
\end{equation*}
Lemma~\ref{bp:p1:iteration} gives $y_m\to0$. Since $T_{\rho/2}\subset T_{\rho_m}$ for every $m$,
$\abs{\Delta(\theta)}\le y_m$ for $\theta\in T_{\rho/2}$ and every $m$, so $\Delta=0$ on $T_{\rho/2}$.

\proofstep{Conclusion}\label{step:p3:g2-conclusion}
Put $\varphi\coloneqq-\psi\in\R^n$. For $\theta\in T_{\rho/2}$, also $\theta+\varphi\in T_{\rho/2}$, and
$\Delta(\theta+\varphi)=0$ reads $K_2(\theta)=K_1(\theta+\varphi)$.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Hypotheses used, and Lean totality]\label{bp:p3:totality-check}
The arguments of this part use from (S1) only that $\mathfrak B$ is open, $h$ is holomorphic on
$\mathfrak B$, and the bounds on $X_h$, $DX_h$ and $D^2X_h$; the results cited from Part~\ref{bp:p2} are
stated under Assumption~\ref{bp:p2:standing}, which (S1)--(S2) contain. The proof does not use the bound
on $Dh$ or $K_2(T_\rho)\subset\mathfrak B$, and it uses $n\ge2$ and $\tau\ge n-1$ only through $n\ge1$ and
$\tau\ge0$ (the cited lemmas of Parts~\ref{bp:p1} and~\ref{bp:p2} hold verbatim for $n\ge1$,
$\tau\ge0$). Concerning
Remark~\ref{bp:p0:totality}: every \Lean{fderiv} is taken at a point of an open set ($T_\rho$ or
$\mathfrak B$) on which the function is complex differentiable, so it is the complex derivative; the
matrix inverse $B$ is used only on $T_\rho$, where (S3) makes the Gram determinant a unit, so the
inverse in \Lean{normalFrame} is the genuine inverse there and is holomorphic (Step~\ref{step:p3:red-holomorphy});
$\avg T^{-1}$ is the genuine inverse by (S3); and every average is the integral over $[0,1]^n$ of a
function continuous on $\R^n$, being holomorphic on a strip that contains $\R^n$ (for $T$, see
Lemma~\ref{bp:p3:torsion-bound}).
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[Deviations from the sources]\label{bp:p3:deviations}
The errata candidates of GJLV05 found while writing this part are recorded in
\texttt{docs/errata/ledger.json}: E-GJLV05-3 (Lemma~14, pp.~884--885: the phase map is not shown to map
its open domain into itself; Lemma~\ref{bp:p3:phase} uses a closed ball of radius $2C_P\eta$),
E-GJLV05-4 (p.~885) and E-GJLV05-5 (Prop.~18, p.~889). Two clarifications, which are not errata. GJLV05
Remark~7 (p.~865) refers to a reduction of flows to the time-one map; for a flow-Diophantine frequency
the time-one rotation can be resonant ($\omega=(1,\sqrt2)$, $k=e_1$), so a reduction must use a time-$t$
map with constants depending on $t$, and we prove the flow version directly. HL19 Remark~3.5 (p.~18)
asserts local uniqueness without proof; for $d=n$ Theorem~\ref{bp:p0:g2} supplies it. The deviations
(D-UNIQ-1 and new) are the following.
(a) We linearize at $K_1$, the torus carrying the
nondegeneracy bounds, while GJLV05 linearize at $K_2$. (b) The phase is fixed once
(Lemma~\ref{bp:p3:phase}); GJLV05 recompute it at each step. (c) The constant $c$ is independent of
$\gamma$, through $\gamma\le\Gamma$ (Lemma~\ref{bp:p3:frequency-bound}); GJLV05 Theorem~2 lets $c$
depend on $\gamma$. (d) The small-divisor exponent is $\tau+n$ (decision D12), giving the factor
$\rho^{2n}$ in~\eqref{eq:p3:c}. (e) The conclusion is on $T_{\rho/2}$ only; FdlLS09 extend it to the
full strip by analytic continuation, which we do not need. (f) The phase normalization uses the
tangential coordinate $BL^\top=N^\top\Omega_0$ of the Case~III frame instead of the map frame $T$ of
GJLV05 (38).
In (b), the phases that GJLV05 \S6 (p.~885) obtains by reapplying their Lemma~14 are all equal to the
first one, because the normalization (78) does not depend on the strip and the fixed point of the
contraction is unique.
\end{remark}
