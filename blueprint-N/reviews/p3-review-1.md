# Independent review 1: shared Hamiltonian ODE layer

## Verdict: REVISE

The main existence, uniqueness, continuation, first-exit and barrier arguments are sound. The layer has the generality needed by tracks I, N and P, and its five frozen-predicate reductions have the correct equations and quantifiers. There is, however, a concrete Lean-totality defect in the compact-neighbourhood lemma when the compact set is empty. The external-facts inventory also gives incorrect Picard–Lindelöf declaration names and incorrectly says that Mathlib has no uniform local-existence statement. These are repairable without changing a frozen declaration. The findings below distinguish the substantive empty-set defect from citation and exposition fixes.

## What I recomputed (refutation attempts that failed)

1. **Nonconvex compact restriction.** Boundedness gives ‖F(x_k)−F(y_k)‖≤2M. Violating every integer Lipschitz constant forces ‖x_k−y_k‖→0. A compact subsequence puts both endpoints in one neighbourhood with a local Lipschitz constant, contradiction. The proof needs no convex hull inside W. Start the sequence at k≥1 to avoid the harmless k=0 division.
2. **Periodic cylinders.** After subtracting floor(θ), a max-norm δ≤1 ball is inside [−1,2]ⁿ×K_δ. Translating the **whole ball by one integer vector** preserves both field values and distances; it does not require a globally Lipschitz periodic extension across cell cuts. This yields the per-ball uniform data actually needed.
3. **Short-time confinement.** On the bootstrap set the distance is ≤M(s−t₀)<δ. This strict interior margin permits a continuity step; the mean-value bound extends the same slope M. The endpoint supremum argument is valid even though F itself need not be continuous in this lemma, since the curve has the asserted derivatives and its derivative norm is bounded while it remains in the ball.
4. **Uniform local time.** With M′=max(M,1) and ε=δ/M′, Picard supplies a closed-interval within-derivative. Interior points give ordinary derivatives. The short-time lemma then places the entire open-interval solution strictly inside the ball and therefore W. No contraction-time inequality was lost.
5. **Arbitrary-interval uniqueness.** For each target time, the two trajectory images of the intervening closed interval form a compact set inside W. The common Lipschitz bound and right/left uniqueness theorem give equality there. This covers endpoints, half-open intervals, singleton intervals and unbounded competitors by compact pieces.
6. **Extension and maximality.** A local solution through an included endpoint extends past that endpoint. At the splice, one-sided derivatives coincide with F(y(b)); the half-line union lemma gives a genuine two-sided derivative. Extending both included endpoints gives an open interval. The union of all open intervals through zero is connected and open; uniqueness makes the glued solution locally equal to an actual solution. The representative outside the maximal interval is arbitrary.
7. **Continuation.** A point t₁ sufficiently near a finite right endpoint has the same uniform local existence time ε as every point in Φ. The new solution agrees on the overlap and extends beyond the alleged endpoint. Closedness of Φ is unnecessary; uniform data is sufficient. In particular lifted angles need not themselves remain in a compact set.
8. **Closed-time confinement glue.** If T_+≤τ, the a-priori estimate confines the maximal solution on [0,T_+), and uniform continuation gives a contradiction. Thus T_+>τ and T_-<−τ, not merely weak inequalities. The exhibited solution is defined and differentiable on a neighbourhood of each endpoint ±τ, exactly as the frozen HasDerivAt predicates demand.
9. **First exit.** If a forward exit exists, choose one actual exit time t₁∈J. The preimage of the closed complement in compact [0,t₁] has a positive minimum. This avoids any implicit assumption that J itself is closed. The minimum is a boundary point by continuity from below, and the supremum formula uses only t∈J. The backward claim follows by time reversal.
10. **Barrier.** At first exit, z(t*) belongs to ∂V∩G⊆S. Two-sided invariance on the whole comparison interval forces z(0)∈S, contradiction. Reversal preserves the invariant-set property. No transversality or distance-to-boundary estimate is missing from this topological statement.
11. **Hamiltonian cancellation.** For ℓ=DH, summing ℓ over coordinate pairs gives ℓ(X_H)=Σ_i(∂_{I_i}H)(∂_{θ_i}H)−Σ_i(∂_{θ_i}H)(∂_{I_i}H)=0. Pointwise differentiability of H along the curve suffices for chain rule and constancy; global C² is not required for this energy theorem.
12. **Newtonian uniform data.** At max-norm distance δ=ρ/8, a pair displacement changes in Euclidean length by at most 2sqrt(2)δ<ρ/2. Thus all pair distances remain ≥ρ/2. For w/ℓ³, the derivative is ℓ⁻³Id−3ℓ⁻⁵wwᵀ; its Euclidean norm is ≤4ℓ⁻³, with fixed dimension factors for max norms. The force itself is bounded by ℓ⁻². Consequently F and DF have bounds independent of absolute position whenever velocities and inverse collision distances are controlled. Fixed-center and rotating restricted variants add exactly the origin exclusions or bounded linear terms described in the text.
