## What I recomputed (refutation attempts that failed)

1. For R,s>0 the real tube equals B(0,R+s). One inclusion is the triangle inequality. Conversely split a vector of norm <R+s as J=R/(R+s) I and I−J=s/(R+s) I; both strict inequalities follow. This also works in dimension zero. The complex tube is generally not a complex max-norm ball of radius R+s; Part 0 correctly only asserts the identity for its real slice.
2. Size zero forces f=0 on the open complex domain. Hence its real derivative is zero near every point of the integrable orbit, and X_{H_{h,f}}=(ω_h(I₀),0). The affine orbit derivative agrees, and I₀∈B(0,R/2)⊂B(0,R). This gives all of N-ZERO, with no negative power at ε=0.
3. For N-QC, the derivative is `(v,u)↦(ω·v,uω+λQv)`. A kernel vector satisfies ω·v=0; dotting the second component with v gives λQ[v,v]=0. Since λm>0, QC forces ‖v‖∞=0, hence v=0; then uω=0 and ω≠0 force u=0. Equal finite dimensions give bijectivity. C² on open U makes all derivatives genuine. At n=0 the nonzero-frequency premise is impossible; the theorem is vacuous, not false.
4. The complex nested operator norm bounds each Hessian entry by M_C by testing unit coordinate vectors. The entry bound implies Euclidean matrix operator norm ≤nM_C, via Cauchy–Schwarz (also valid for complex entries). A complex symmetric matrix need not have operator norm equal to spectral radius; the frozen definition and Part 0 correctly avoid that replacement.
5. The quantitative conversion works after N05's correction. Put q=m/n, B_c=nM_C+q, μ=q/2 and ell=ω_min q/(8(B_c+q)). For a Euclidean unit v with |ω·v|≤ell, decompose v=u+te, e=ω/‖ω‖₂, u⊥ω. Then |t|≤q/(8(B_c+q)), Q[u,u]≥q‖u‖₂² and |Q|≤B_c. Thus Q[v,v]≥q−(q+B_c)t²−2B_c|t|≥q−q/8−q/4≥μ. Homogeneity gives the required conditional inequality, not full positivity.
6. The period conversion H̃(φ,I)=2πH(φ/(2π),I) preserves physical time: φ′=2π∂_I H and I′=−∂_θH=−∂_φH̃. Merely rescaling the angle without the Hamiltonian multiplier would fail. M, M_C, curvature and frequency bounds scale by 2π.
7. Using the choices in `sources.md`, r₀≤min(s/2,R/8) puts the complex Euclidean tube of the closed max-norm R/2 ball inside the frozen complex domain and its real slice inside B(0,3R/4). The additional r₀≤4ω_min/μ makes the source exceptional threshold (2πμ)r₀/8≤πω_min, below initial source frequencies ≥2πω_min. The upper bound ‖ω̃‖₂≤2πsqrt(n)M≤2πnM gives a uniform positive time prefactor. No bound on h's value A is needed.
8. The fixed Fourier width loss has s₀+2σ=3πs/2<2πs and perturbation bound Fε with F=2πcoth^n(πs/4). Pöschel's exponent a=1/(2n) then gives C=(r₀/A_src)(F/ε*)^a and c=(s₀/6)(ε*/F)^a. Shrinking ε₀ so Cε₀^a≤R/4 keeps every initial I₀∈B(0,R/2) within B(0,3R/4). The constants are chosen from the seven frozen inputs before h,f,ε and initial data. These are mathematical conversions, not a fresh formal proof of Pöschel's theorem.

## Source defects and deviations for handoff

- BM11 p.4 uses max norms for QC(m) and coordinate derivative bounds, with Euclidean orthogonality: Part 0 transcribes this correctly. Pöschel's p.190 Hessian bound is complex and Euclidean, and p.191 has the low-frequency exception; Part 0 correctly keeps these source-route additions visible rather than pretending they belong to original N-HEAD.
- Pöschel p.190 says the norm of a symmetric Q “amounts to the spectral radius norm”, although Q has been allowed at complex actions. This is false for complex symmetric matrices in general: Q=[[1,i],[i,−1]] is nonzero but Q²=0, so its spectral radius is zero and its operator norm is positive. The frozen operator-bound definition and the blueprint correctly use operator norms; report the source qualification to the orchestrator, without weakening that premise.
- BM11 p.9's unrestricted stable image-ball constants for all positive λ are outside frozen N-QC; only the pointwise derivative assertion is transcribed. This review does not rely on that quantitative assertion or claim a fresh full audit of it.
- Declared adaptations include period-one lifts, the non-strict size bound (BM11's strict condition can be applied with 2ε for ε>0), the separate zero theorem, and the strengthened-input headline. No new mathematical deviation or frozen edit was introduced by this review. The PLAN's convention departure is reported in N07, not silently adopted.
