There is also an incorrect inequality in that consumer's proof sketch, recorded below as an interface finding rather than a defect in N's composition theorem.

## Exact refutation and repaired bracket calculation

Take n=2, m=1, D={−2,2}, r0=1/4. The two action discs are disjoint. On the first disc put `u(θ,I)=cos(2πθ₁)`, on the second put `u(θ,I)=cos(2πθ₂)`. This is jointly holomorphic and periodic on the stated domain and real at real points. Its norm is `|u|_(r0,s0)=exp(2πs0)`. Each global derivative norm is `2π exp(2πs)`, because its supremum is attained on its own component. Choose s>0 and `s0−s=1/(2π)`. The claimed left side is `4π exp(2πs)`; its claimed right side is `2π exp(2πs)`. Any r with 0<r<r0 satisfies the remaining premises. Disconnected D is explicitly allowed, including for the P use.

For B.4, at fixed I put t=s−σ. The pointwise product estimate gives

`|{u,v}|_(I,t) ≤ (Σ_i |∂θ_i u|_(I,t)) max_i |∂I_i v|_(I,t) + max_i |∂I_i u|_(I,t) (Σ_i |∂θ_i v|_(I,t))`.

The angular factors are at most `|u|_(I,s0)/(e(s0−s+σ))` and `|v|_(I,s)/(eσ)`. The action factors are at most `|v|_(r,s)/ρ` and `|u|_(r0,s0)/(r0−r+ρ)`, respectively, by coefficientwise Cauchy integrals. Now bound the remaining pointwise input norms by their global norms and take the supremum. This proves exactly the displayed B.4 constant. Pöschel p.214 explicitly starts that proof with “Fix I”, which is the essential order here.

## What I recomputed: refutations that failed

1. **Domains and Fourier norm.** The existential action tube works for arbitrary D, not only a ball, a connected set or a convex set. The closed polydiscs fit because the starting point has strict room inside the smaller tube/strip. The coefficient-radius convention is `2πs`, whereas s is geometric width. Uniform convergence on every smaller strip follows from the summable majorant `S exp(−2π|k|₁(s−s'))`. Coefficients, products and projections respect restriction to smaller action tubes. No finiteness of the norm at the original strip follows merely from holomorphy; the statements correctly assume finite norm where needed.
2. **B.1 and projections.** Summing the coefficient decay yields `Σ_k exp(−2π|k|₁σ)=coth(πσ)^n`, including vector/matrix coefficient max norms. Tail decay is `exp(−2πKσ)`; weakening it to `exp(−Kσ)` is legitimate. The algebra property is pointwise before supremum. In the action Cauchy estimate, summation can be passed through the contour integral by Tonelli; it is not replaced by a sum of independent suprema.
3. **Dictionary `bpN:p4:dictionary`.** For the source Hamiltonian pulled back under `φ=2πθ, I_P=I/(2π)`, the form is preserved and the Hamiltonian values and time are unchanged. Source widths/losses are `s_P=2πs`, `σ_P=2πσ`, `r_P=r/(2π)`, `ρ_P=ρ/(2π)`, so `ρ_Pσ_P=ρσ`. Directional derivatives scale inversely to their coordinates. Mixed bracket denominators and the scaled displacement/operator norms are invariant. B.1's half-strip parameter is π times the *total geometric strip loss*; it is not the σ_P of a full loss in B.3. The text spells out this distinction in its B.1 formula. The alternative fixed-action conversion multiplies the Hamiltonian/generator by 2π and gives the same ratio. Thus the dictionary itself introduces no extra 2π in η. The claim of literal source fidelity must still be corrected for N45-01, which is not a normalization issue.
4. **Small-field flow.** If a maximal endpoint were ≤1, speed ≤ε keeps the whole terminal tail in the compact closed ball of radius ε≤1/2 inside Ω; p3 compact continuation contradicts that endpoint. The same proof works backwards. Around each initial point, a slightly larger compact ball inside Ω gives a uniform Lipschitz constant Λ. Picard differences have bound `ε Λ^k |t|^(k+1)/(k+1)!`, uniformly in a local initial-point ball. This gives the holomorphic flow and, on a smaller ball, uniform derivative convergence and the variational equation. The inverse identities follow from uniqueness. Applying C.1 to `X^t−id` on a radius-1/2 ball gives `|DX^t−Id|ν≤2ε`. Reality follows by running this same real ODE argument on the real slice; no conjugation invariance of arbitrary Ω or ν is required. The phrase “apply (a) to E=R^N” is understood as reusing its proof via p3, since the displayed lemma is formulated over C^N.
5. **Sign and symplecticity.** With angle-first order, `J=[[0,Id],[-Id,0]]` is the negative of frozen Ω₀, and `Xφ=J∇φ` is exactly the frozen field. The bracket is `Du Xv`; in particular `{φ,h}=∂θφ·ω` and `{h,φ}=−ω·∂θφ`. For a symmetric complex Hessian S, `DF=JS` gives `DFᵀJ+JDF=0`. Plain transpose, not adjoint, yields `YᵀJY=J` and `YJYᵀ=J`. These prove bracket invariance and `DΦ X_(H∘Φ)=X_H∘Φ`. The weaker assertion det Y=±1 is valid (continuity in fact gives +1).
6. **Taylor remainder.** The N-th remainder kernel is `(1−t)^(N−1)/(N−1)!`; its integral is 1/N!. Taking N=2 for h and N=1 for f₀ gives exactly the p.211 formula, with the displayed signs. All derivatives/integrals are taken on actual open holomorphy domains and compact time intervals, so total derivatives or nonintegrable Bochner integrals are not used as substitutes for these facts.
7. **Adjoint constants.** With κ=ρ/h and λ=σ/h, the i-th bracket factor is `2/[e(h+i)κλ]`. Multiplication gives `(2h²/(eρσ))^h h!/(2h)!`. Dividing by h! and using `(2h)!≥(2h/e)^(2h)` gives `(eη/4)^h`. The domain differences `r'−r+iκ≥(h+i)κ` and their angular counterparts have the correct sign and include the equality r'=r+ρ.
8. **B.5 equality, including η=1/2.** Put ε=η/2≤1/4. Every trajectory stays in the domain with losses `(1−ε)(ρ,σ)`. The adjoint estimate there uses `hatη=η/(1−ε)^2≤8/9`. Consequently the Taylor remainder is bounded by `(e hatη/4)^N |u|≤(2e/9)^N |u|→0`; numerically the ratio is ≤0.604063. This proves equality with the Lie series, not merely convergence of a formal series. The norm bound is first `(1−eη/4)^−1`, then the stated `(1−η)^−1`. Negative times use tφ as well. The proof does not establish the full source range η<1 and does not claim to do so.
9. **Transform and map package.** An infinite weighted norm of h is harmless when `w={h,φ}` has finite norm: Taylor derivatives of h from order one are iterated brackets of w. The extra factorial gives the coefficient `1/(j+1)` in the remainder bound, hence `η/(1−η)(|w|/2+|f₀|)`. The parameter-integral corollary has enough joint continuity for holomorphy and Fubini. The map domains r−3ρ/2 and r−2ρ, the displacement components `|φ|/ρ`, `|φ|/(eσ)`, the operator scaling W, and the two inverse identities all follow with ε≤1/2. Composition estimates additionally require η≤1/2, i.e. ε≤1/4; these two thresholds must not be conflated at consumers.

### The additional norm and width mismatch at P

Writing Σ for P’s coefficient radius, the corresponding N geometric width is s=Σ/(2π). In general

`sup_I Σ_k |f_k(I)| e^(Σ|k|₁) ≤ Σ_k sup_I |f_k(I)| e^(Σ|k|₁)`.

The reverse inequality with constant 1 fails: on M disjoint action components put a single distinct mode k_j on component j, normalized to weighted coefficient size 1, and zero for that mode on every other component. This defines a holomorphic function on the disconnected action tube. N’s norm is 1, while P’s is M. There is no uniform dimension-only equivalence. Consequently composition bounded in N’s norm cannot simply be promoted to bounded composition in P’s norm. The same issue applies to the integral remainder estimate.

The stronger norm does admit a plausible parallel proof: use sup-action coefficient sequences throughout the convolution estimate and coefficientwise Cauchy bounds, then repeat the bracket/adjoint/Taylor argument in that norm. It needs an explicit labelled statement and proof. Merely citing N’s existing theorem and observing that P’s input norm is larger proves only a weaker **N-norm** bound on the output.

For the widths, a P loss ΔΣ corresponds to a geometric loss ΔΣ/(2π), so N’s angular derivative constant becomes `2π/(eΔΣ)` and its tail becomes `exp(−KΔΣ)`. The generator χ_k=f_k/(2π i k·ω) cancels the extra 2π in the mixed estimates, but the bookkeeping must be made explicitly. In particular N’s composition parameter is `η_N=4π|χ|_N/(ρ ΔΣ)` when ρ is the unchanged action loss. This is a genuine interface conversion, not the substitution s_N=Σ.
